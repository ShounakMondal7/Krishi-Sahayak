ICAR TECHNICAL BULLETIN — Sugarcane Cultivation Guide for Maharashtra, UP, and Tamil Nadu
Source: ICAR-Sugarcane Breeding Institute (SBI), Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu
Document Reference: ICAR-SBI/Tech/2024/MH-UP-TN-01

India is the world's second-largest sugarcane producer after Brazil. Uttar Pradesh (largest), Maharashtra,
Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, and Andhra Pradesh are the major producing states. India's sugar industry employs
over 50 million people.

SECTION 1: RECOMMENDED VARIETIES — STATE-WISE
=======================================================================

UTTAR PRADESH:
1. Co 0238 (Karan 4): Most popular in UP
   - Duration: 12 months | Yield: 80–90 t/ha
   - High sucrose (14.5%) | Upright cane, good ratoon
   - Early maturing — good for sugar mills
   
2. Co 0118: Good for waterlogged conditions
   - Yield: 75–85 t/ha

3. CoLk 94184: Short duration (10 months)
   - Ideal for late planting

MAHARASHTRA:
1. Co 86032 (Phule): Most grown in Maharashtra
   - Yield: 100–120 t/ha (irrigated)
   - High juice quality, early maturing
   
2. CoM 265: Short duration variety
   
3. Co 8014: Late variety, high yield (110–130 t/ha)

TAMIL Nadu / ANDHRA PRADESH:
1. Co 86032: Widely adapted
2. CoC 671: High sucrose content (15%)
3. CoSi 95071: Drought tolerant, suitable for Deccan plateau

KARNATAKA:
1. Co 92005 (ULM): Best for tropical conditions
2. CoC 671: High sucrose

SECTION 2: PLANTING SEASONS
=======================================================================

| State          | Planting Season         | Harvest Season      |
|----------------|------------------------|---------------------|
| UP (Spring)    | Feb 15 – March 30      | Nov – Jan (12 m)    |
| UP (Autumn)    | Sep 15 – Oct 15        | Nov – Jan (14–15 m) |
| Maharashtra    | Oct – Nov (Suru)        | Oct – Feb (12–14 m) |
| Maharashtra    | Jan – Feb (Adsali)      | Jan – March (18 m)  |
| Tamil Nadu     | Nov – Jan               | Nov – Dec next year |
| Karnataka      | Aug – Oct               | Aug – Oct next year |

PLANTING METHODS:
1. Ring Pit Method (Best for Maharashtra):
   - Pits: 60 cm × 60 cm × 60 cm
   - 3 sett/pit, 2-bud setts
   - Spacing: 1.2 m between rows, 0.6 m between pits
   - Benefit: 30% water saving, better ratoon management

2. Flat Bed (Most common, UP):
   - Furrow spacing: 90 cm (UP) / 75 cm (TN)
   - Depth of furrows: 20–25 cm
   - 2-3 bud setts end-to-end in furrows

3. Trash Mulching Method:
   - Trash from previous crop used as mulch
   - Reduces weeding cost, saves moisture

SEED MATERIAL:
- Select disease-free seed from seed nurseries
- Use top 3rd of cane for setts (most vigorous buds)
- Sett treatment: Carbendazim 0.1% + Emisan (agrosan) 2g/litre soak 30 minutes
- Hot Water Treatment: 50°C for 2 hours — kills ratoon stunting disease (RSD)

SECTION 3: FERTILIZER MANAGEMENT
=======================================================================

RECOMMENDED DOSE (per hectare):

| State         | N       | P₂O₅   | K₂O     | Note                |
|--------------|---------|--------|--------|---------------------|
| UP           | 250 kg  | 60 kg  | 60 kg  | 3 splits for N      |
| Maharashtra  | 250 kg  | 115 kg | 115 kg | Ring pit method      |
| Tamil Nadu   | 275 kg  | 62 kg  | 62 kg  |                     |

SPLIT APPLICATION (UP example, 250 kg N/ha):
- At planting: 1/3 N + full P + full K
- 30 DAS (earthing up): 1/3 N
- 90 DAS: 1/3 N

BIOFERTILIZERS (reduce chemical N by 25%):
- Azospirillum: 10 kg/ha soil application
- Phosphobacteria: 10 kg/ha
- Combined use reduces N requirement from 250 to 188 kg/ha

FOLIAR NUTRITION:
- 2% Urea spray at 3 and 5 months
- 1% KCl spray at 5–6 months (improves sugar content)
- Silica (SiO₂) 200 kg/ha as slag: improves stem strength, pest resistance

=======================================================================
SECTION 4: WEED MANAGEMENT
=======================================================================

CRITICAL WEED-FREE PERIOD: First 90 days after planting

PRE-EMERGENCE:
- Atrazine 50% WP: 2 kg/ha in 600 litres water (1–2 DAS)

POST-EMERGENCE (for narrow-leaf weeds):
- 2,4-D sodium salt 80%: 1.5 kg/ha at 30–45 DAS

POST-EMERGENCE (broad-spectrum):
- Metribuzin 70% WP: 1 kg/ha at 15–20 DAS

TRASH MULCHING: Suppresses 70% of weeds naturally — recommended

SECTION 5: MAJOR DISEASES OF SUGARCANE
=======================================================================

5.1 RED ROT (Colletotrichum falcatum)
MOST SERIOUS DISEASE of sugarcane in India.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Cut the cane longitudinally: red discolouration of internal tissue
- White spots at irregular intervals on the red tissue
- Characteristic sour/alcoholic smell from infected cane
- Leaves dry up prematurely

MANAGEMENT:
- Use disease-free seed from certified sources
- Hot water treatment (50°C/2 hours) of setts
- Sett treatment: Carbendazim 0.1% soak for 5 minutes
- Grow resistant varieties: CoJ 64, Co 0238 (resistant in UP)
- Remove and destroy infected clumps

5.2 RATOON STUNTING DISEASE (RSD) — Leifsonia xyli
IDENTIFICATION:
- No specific visible symptoms
- Stunted plant, fewer tillers
- Cut node — orange-yellow discolouration at nodal junction

MANAGEMENT:
- Hot water treatment of setts (MANDATORY for ratoon)
- No chemical control effective after infection

5.3 WILT (Fusarium sacchari)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Internal drying of cane
- Hollow cavity with dried pith
- Pink/red discolouration inside

MANAGEMENT:
- Crop rotation (avoid sugarcane monoculture)
- Use healthy, wilt-tolerant varieties
- Carbendazim 0.1% sett treatment

5.4 SMUT (Ustilago scitaminea)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Long black whip-like structure emerging from top of shoot
- Reduces yield by 30–90%

MANAGEMENT:
- Grow resistant varieties: Co 8014, Co 86032 (resistant)
- Destroy infected plants immediately
- Hot water sett treatment

SECTION 6: MAJOR PESTS OF SUGARCANE
=======================================================================

6.1 EARLY SHOOT BORER (Chilo infuscatellus)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Dead heart in young plants (central leaf withers)
- Maggot inside the central shoot
- Up to 30–40% dead hearts in epidemic years

MANAGEMENT:
- Release egg parasitoid Trichogramma chilonis: 50,000/ha twice (at 45 and 60 DAS)
- Chlorpyrifos 20 EC: 3 litres + sand 20 kg/ha in furrows at planting
- Phorate 10G: 15 kg/ha in furrows

6.2 TOP BORER (Scirpophaga excerptalis)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Attacks growing point — dead heart at tillering/grand growth stage
- Caterpillar found inside top portion of stalk

MANAGEMENT:
- Chlorpyrifos 20 EC: 500 ml/acre at 90–120 DAS
- Fipronil 0.3% GR: 7.5 kg/ha

6.3 WOOLLY APHID (Ceratovacuna lanigera)
IDENTIFICATION:
- White cottony mass on leaf underside
- Ants herding the colonies
- Severe stunting and sooty mould

MANAGEMENT:
- Spray water jets to dislodge colonies
- Chlorpyrifos 20 EC: 1.5 litres/acre
- Endosulfan (if approved by state)

6.4 WHITE GRUBS (Holotrichia serrata)
IDENTIFICATION:
- C-shaped grubs in soil eating roots
- Plants wilt in patches (circular patches of dying cane)

MANAGEMENT:
- Deep summer ploughing (exposes grubs to birds/sun)
- Chlorpyrifos 20 EC: 3 litres drench in soil

=======================================================================
SECTION 7: RATOON MANAGEMENT
=======================================================================

Ratoon crop saves: Rs 15,000–20,000/ha vs replanting

KEY STEPS:
1. Root stripping and stubble shaving (within 10 days of harvest)
2. Gap filling with fresh setts in empty patches
3. Intercultivation + earthing up at 30 DAS
4. 75% of main crop fertilizer dose
5. Irrigate within 2–3 days of harvest
6. Propiconazole spray: To prevent ratoon diseases

Maximum 2 ratoons per planting (yield declines significantly after 2nd ratoon)
