ICAR COMPREHENSIVE ALLIED ENTERPRISES: MUSHROOM, BEEKEEPING & SERICULTURE
================================================================================
Grounded in ICAR-DMR (Directorate of Mushroom Research, Solan), ICAR-KVIC, & CSB Norms.

SECTION 1: COMMERCIAL MUSHROOM CULTIVATION (BUTTON, OYSTER & MILKY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Mushroom cultivation converts agricultural lignocellulosic waste (paddy straw, wheat straw, sugarcane bagasse) into protein-rich human food (20-35% crude protein on dry weight basis) inside controlled indoor environments, requiring zero arable land and providing continuous daily cash flow.

1. White Button Mushroom (Agaricus bisporus - Seasonal Winter / Year-Round AC):
   - Compost Formulation (Short Method of Composting in Bulk Pasteurization Tunnel):
     * Wheat straw (1000 kg) + Poultry manure (400 kg) + Wheat bran (100 kg) + Urea (15 kg) + Gypsum (40 kg).
     * Phase I (Outdoor Fermentation): 10-12 days; thorough wetting and periodic turning.
     * Phase II (Indoor Peak Heating & Conditioning): Pasteurize compost mass at 58°C to 60°C for 6 to 8 hours to eliminate nematodes, weed fungi (Coprinus, Trichoderma), and insect eggs; conditioned at 48°C to 50°C for 3-4 days until ammonia scent drops to <10 ppm, pH stabilizes at 7.2 to 7.6, and moisture reaches 68-70%.
   - Spawning & Spawn Run:
     * High-quality grain spawn (produced on wheat/jowar grains) mixed thoroughly into cool compost @ 0.7% to 1.0% by weight (700g-1000g spawn per 100 kg compost).
     * Fill compost into 10 kg polybags (thickness 100 gauge). Place on GI tiered racks.
     * Spawn Run Conditions: Maintain room temperature strictly at 22°C to 24°C and relative humidity (RH) at 85-90%. Keep room dark with closed ventilation (high CO2 5,000-10,000 ppm promotes vigorous white mycelial spread). Mycelium colonizes compost completely in 12 to 14 days.
   - Casing Layer & Cropping:
     * Casing Mixture: Decomposed Coir pith + 2-year-old Farmyard Manure (FYM) in 1:1 ratio + Agricultural Lime (CaCO3) to adjust pH to 7.8-8.2. Steam pasteurize at 65°C for 4 hours or drench with 2% Formalin.
     * Apply 3.5 to 4.0 cm uniform casing layer over colonized compost.
     * Pinhead Induction: After 8 days of casing, drop room temperature to 16°C to 18°C, introduce fresh filtered air (drop CO2 to 800-1,200 ppm), and spray fine mist water. Tiny white pinheads form within 5 to 7 days.
     * Harvesting: Pick tight closed button mushrooms (cap diameter 3-4 cm) by gentle twisting. Never pull or cut with dirty blades. 100 kg compost yields 18 to 22 kg fresh button mushrooms over 3 flushes.

2. Oyster Mushroom / Dhingri (Pleurotus ostreatus / P. florida / P. sajor-caju):
   - Adaptability: Easiest mushroom for beginners; grows at 22°C to 28°C and 80-85% RH; ideal for tropical and sub-tropical India from October to March.
   - Substrate Preparation: Chop clean fresh paddy or wheat straw into 2-3 cm bits. Soak in water containing 7.5g Carbendazim + 100 ml Formalin per 100 liters water for 16-18 hours (chemical sterilization), or boil in hot water at 80°C for 1 hour. Drain excess water until moisture is 65% (no dripping when squeezed in palm).
   - Bagging & Spawning: Layered spawning in perforated 40 x 60 cm polythene bags @ 2.5% spawn rate. Hang or stack in well-ventilated dark room for 15 days until white mycelium covers the bag.
   - Slitting & Cropping: Slit polythene bag completely; hang cylinders; mist water 2-3 times daily. Shelf mushrooms develop in clusters within 3-4 days. Yield: 700g to 900g fresh oyster mushrooms per 1 kg dry straw.

3. Milky Mushroom (Calocybe indica - High Summer Performer):
   - Thrives in scorching summer temperatures of 30°C to 38°C; pure milky white, thick crunchy stem, and long shelf life (4-5 days without refrigeration).

SECTION 2: SCIENTIFIC APICULTURE (COMMERCIAL BEEKEEPING)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Beekeeping increases cross-pollination in mustard, sunflower, apple, litchi, and pulses, boosting farm yields by 20% to 35% while yielding pure honey, beeswax, royal jelly, propolis, and bee venom.
- Honeybee Species Selection:
  * Apis mellifera (Italian Honeybee): Industry standard for commercial migratory beekeeping; gentle temperament, high brood rearing, low swarming tendency, and high honey yield (25 to 40 kg per colony annually).
  * Apis cerana indica (Indian Indigenous Bee): Adapted to hilly/forested tracts; yields 8 to 12 kg honey per hive annually.
- Equipment Specifications: Standard BIS Langstroth 10-frame beehive (bottom board, brood chamber with frames, queen excluder wire grid, super chamber for honey storage, inner cover, and telescopic galvanized iron roof).
- Migratory Floral Calendar (North-West India):
  * October to February: Mustard & Rapeseed (Rajasthan, Haryana, Western UP) — heavy honey flow and massive colony division.
  * March to April: Eucalyptus, Shisham, and Citrus (Punjab, UP, Himachal).
  * May to June: Litchi (Dehradun, Muzaffarpur, Ramnagar) and Sunflower.
  * July to September: Dearth period (monsoon); feed artificial sugar syrup (1:1 sugar:water) + pollen substitute (roasted soybean flour + skimmed milk powder + brewer's yeast) to sustain colonies.
- Bee Diseases & Parasite Management:
  * Varroa Mite (Varroa destructor): Blood-sucking external parasite on pupae and adult bees causing wing deformity. Treat using Food-Grade Formic Acid (85%) @ 5 ml per pad placed on brood frame top bars OR vaporize 2-3g Oxalic Acid dihydrate. Never use synthetic chemical acaricides during active honey flow!
  * Wax Moth (Galleria mellonella): Clean bottom boards weekly; expose vacant frames to direct sunlight.

SECTION 3: SERICULTURE (MULBERRY SILKWORM REARING)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sericulture integrates mulberry fodder cultivation with rearing of silkworm caterpillars (Bombyx mori) for silk cocoons.
- Mulberry Cultivation: Plant high-yielding varieties (V-1, S-36, G-4) at 90 x 90 cm or paired row (150+90) x 60 cm. 1 acre mulberry yields 20 to 25 tonnes tender leaves annually, sufficient to rear 800 to 1,000 disease-free layings (DFLs) of bivoltine silkworms.
- Silkworm Rearing Management:
  * Chawki Rearing (1st & 2nd Instar): Feed young tender chopped succulent leaves at 26-28°C and 80-85% RH.
  * Late Age Rearing (3rd, 4th & 5th Instar): Feed mature whole shoot branches twice daily on shoot-rearing racks at 24-25°C and 70% RH. Maintain strict cleanliness using Vijetha / Sanitech bleaching lime dust after bed cleaning.
  * Spinning & Mountages: Transfer mature ripe golden-yellow worms to plastic collapsible mountages (Netrikes). Worms spin silk cocoons in 48-72 hours.
  * Cocoon Harvest: Harvest on 6th day after spinning. 100 DFLs produce 70 to 85 kg bivoltine cocoons valued at ₹450 to ₹650 per kg at government silk cocoon markets (Ramanagara, Malavalli).
- Central Silk Board (CSB) & State Sericulture Subsidies: 50% to 75% subsidy for constructing modern silkworm rearing sheds (up to ₹2.5 Lakh) and purchasing rearing equipment.
