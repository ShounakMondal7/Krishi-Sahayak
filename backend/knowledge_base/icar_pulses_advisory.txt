ICAR TECHNICAL BULLETIN — Pulses (Dal) Cultivation Advisory for India
Source: ICAR-Indian Institute of Pulses Research (IIPR), Kanpur, Uttar Pradesh
Document Reference: ICAR-IIPR/Tech/2024/IN-01

India is the world's largest producer AND consumer of pulses (dal). The major pulses grown in India are:
- Chickpea (Gram / Chana) — 40% of total pulse area
- Pigeon Pea (Tur / Arhar) — 25%
- Lentil (Masoor) — 10%
- Black Gram (Urad) — 10%
- Green Gram (Moong) — 10%

India faces a chronic pulse deficit and imports 2–3 million tonnes annually.
Improving pulse productivity is a national priority under the National Food Security Mission.

=======================================================================
SECTION 1: CHICKPEA (GRAM / CHANA) — Cicer arietinum
=======================================================================

1.1 RECOMMENDED VARIETIES

DESI CHICKPEA (small brown seed — most grown):
1. JG 11 (Madhya Pradesh):
   - Duration: 90–95 days | Yield: 25–30 q/ha
   - Fusarium wilt and dry root rot resistant
   - Suitable for vertisols (black soils)

2. Pusa 372 (IARI, Delhi):
   - Yield: 20–25 q/ha | Duration: 100 days
   - Suitable for North India (UP, Bihar, MP)

3. Vijay (K 850): Wilt-resistant | UP, Bihar

4. GNG 1958 (Rajasthan): Drought tolerant

KABULI CHICKPEA (large white seed — premium market):
1. Pusa 1003: Yield 25–30 q/ha | White seed, bold
2. PKV Kabuli-2 (Maharashtra): High yielding

1.2 SOWING AND AGRONOMY
- Season: Rabi (October–November)
- Optimal sowing: 25 Oct – 25 Nov
- Spacing: 30 cm × 10 cm
- Seed rate: 75–80 kg/ha (Desi) | 100 kg/ha (Kabuli)
- Rhizobium inoculation: Strain Mesorhizobium ciceri — 200 g/10 kg seed

1.3 FERTILIZER:
- N: 20 kg/ha (starter dose — basal)
- P₂O₅: 40–60 kg/ha (basal)
- K₂O: 30 kg/ha (basal)
- S: 20 kg/ha (as gypsum)

1.4 CHICKPEA DISEASES

FUSARIUM WILT (Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. ciceris):
- Yellowing and wilting of plant (looks like drought)
- Reddish-brown vascular discolouration inside root/stem
MANAGEMENT:
- Use resistant varieties: JG 11, ICC 4814, WR 315
- Trichoderma viride seed treatment 4 g/kg seed
- Crop rotation (avoid chickpea-chickpea)

ASCOCHYTA BLIGHT (Ascochyta rabiei):
- Dark water-soaked lesions on leaves, stems, pods
- Entire plant blackens in epidemic (late February–March rains)
MANAGEMENT:
- Mancozeb 75 WP: 2 g/litre spray at first appearance
- Tebuconazole 25.9 EC: 1 ml/litre (curative)
- Chlorothalonil 75 WP: 2 g/litre

BOTRYTIS GREY MOULD:
- Grey mould on stem and pods in humid conditions
MANAGEMENT: Carbendazim 0.1% spray

1.5 CHICKPEA PESTS

POD BORER (Helicoverpa armigera):
- MOST IMPORTANT PEST of chickpea — can cause 50–80% yield loss
- ETL: 1 larva per plant
MANAGEMENT:
- Pheromone traps: 5 traps/ha (Helilure)
- Chickpea is a unique host — NPV (HaNPV) is highly effective
  Apply HaNPV: 500 LE/ha spray in evening
- Emamectin benzoate 5 SG: 200 g/acre
- Chlorantraniliprole 18.5 SC: 150 ml/acre
- Indoxacarb 15.8 EC: 333 ml/acre

APHIDS (Aphis craccivora):
- Black aphids on growing tips
MANAGEMENT: Imidacloprid 17.8 SL 300 ml/ha

SECTION 2: PIGEON PEA (TUR / ARHAR) — Cajanus cajan
=======================================================================

2.1 RECOMMENDED VARIETIES

SHORT DURATION (110–130 days):
1. UPAS 120: Most popular in UP, Bihar
   - Yield: 12–15 q/ha | Kharif crop
2. ICPL 88039: Dwarf, early maturing
3. Prabhat (IARI): Short duration, suitable for intercropping

MEDIUM DURATION (150–180 days):
1. Narendra Arhar 1: UP/Bihar | Yield 15–20 q/ha
2. BSMR 736 (Maharashtra): Fusarium wilt resistant

LONG DURATION (180–240 days):
1. C 11 (Tur): Classic variety, Karnataka
2. PRG 100 (Maharashtra/AP): High yielding

2.2 SOWING
- Kharif sowing: June 15 – July 15
- Spacing: 60–75 cm between rows × 20–25 cm
- Seed rate: 15–20 kg/ha | Rhizobium inoculant applied

2.3 FERTILIZER:
- N: 25 kg/ha (basal only)
- P₂O₅: 50 kg/ha
- K₂O: 20–25 kg/ha
- Rhizobium (Bradyrhizobium species): 200 g/10 kg seed

2.4 PIGEON PEA DISEASES

FUSARIUM WILT (Fusarium udum):
- Most serious disease | Up to 90% mortality in endemic areas
- Yellowing from lower leaves | Brown ring in stem vascular tissue
MANAGEMENT:
- Grow resistant varieties: BSMR 736, ICPL 87119 (Asha)
- Trichoderma seed treatment
- Crop rotation (minimum 3-year break from pigeon pea)

STERILITY MOSAIC (caused by mite-vectored virus):
- All leaves become small, green, sterile (no pods)
- Spread by eriophyid mite (Aceria cajani)
MANAGEMENT:
- Dicofol 18.5 EC: 2 ml/litre spray
- Grow resistant varieties: Asha (ICPL 87119)

2.5 PIGEON PEA PESTS

POD FLY (Melanagromyza obtusa):
- Maggot inside the pod, feeding on seed
- Pinholes on pods; seeds become shrivelled
MANAGEMENT:
- Monocrotophos 36 SL: 1 litre/ha at 50% flowering + 15 days later

POD BORER (Helicoverpa + Maruca testulalis):
- Same management as chickpea pod borer

SECTION 3: GREEN GRAM (MOONG) — Vigna radiata
=======================================================================

3.1 VARIETIES
1. Pusa Vishal (IPM 02-3): Short duration 65 days | Yield 15–18 q/ha
2. Samrat (PDM 54): Most popular | Yield 12–15 q/ha
3. Pusa Ratna: MYMV (Yellow Mosaic Virus) resistant
4. SML 668 (Punjab): Short duration, good for spring/summer

3.2 SOWING
- Kharif: June–July | Spring/Summer: Feb–March
- Spacing: 30 cm × 10 cm | Seed rate: 20–25 kg/ha

3.3 DISEASES
YELLOW MOSAIC VIRUS (MYMV):
- Bright yellow patches on leaves (mosaic pattern)
- Spread by whitefly
MANAGEMENT:
- Grow MYMV-resistant varieties: Pusa Ratna, ML 1299
- Imidacloprid seed treatment
- Whitefly control: Imidacloprid 17.8 SL + Dimethoate 30 EC

3.4 PESTS — same as black gram (see below)

=======================================================================
SECTION 4: BLACK GRAM (URAD) — Vigna mungo
=======================================================================

4.1 VARIETIES
1. KU 301: Short duration (70 days) | Yield 15–18 q/ha
2. Pant U 30: Kharif variety | Good quality
3. WBU 108 (West Bengal): Suitable for eastern India

4.2 LEAF CURL VIRUS:
- Leaves curl upward, appear thick and leathery
- Spread by thrips
MANAGEMENT:
- Imidacloprid seed treatment
- Spinosad 45 SC: 75 ml/acre for thrips control

=======================================================================
SECTION 5: LENTIL (MASOOR) — Lens culinaris
=======================================================================

5.1 VARIETIES
1. Pant L 639: Yield 20–25 q/ha | Short duration (120 days)
2. Sabouri (K 75): Long duration | UP, Bihar
3. LL 699 (Punjab): Rust resistant

5.2 RUST (Uromyces viciae-fabae):
- Orange pustules on stem and leaves
MANAGEMENT:
- Mancozeb 75 WP 2 g/litre spray
- Grow rust-resistant varieties: LL 699

SECTION 6: PULSE IMPROVEMENT TIPS
=======================================================================

COMMON MISTAKES:
1. Not using Rhizobium inoculant — losing 40–60 kg/ha free nitrogen
2. Excess nitrogen → vegetative growth, poor pod set
3. Skipping fungicide seed treatment → 20% yield loss from soil diseases
4. Late sowing → 30–50% yield reduction

INTERCROPPING (improves income):
- Pigeon pea + Sorghum/Maize: 2:1 ratio (highly beneficial)
- Chickpea + Mustard: 6:2 row ratio
- Groundnut + Pigeon pea: 4:2 ratio

NATIONAL PULSE MISSION SUPPORT:
- Subsidy on certified seeds: 50% of cost (up to Rs 1,200/ha)
- Free distribution of micronutrients: State government schemes
- Contact: Nearest District Agriculture Office for Pulse Mission support
