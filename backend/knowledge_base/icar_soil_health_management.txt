SECTION 1: UNDERSTANDING SOIL pH
=======================================================================

Soil pH is the single most important soil property that determines nutrient availability.

pH SCALE FOR AGRICULTURE:
- pH < 4.5: Very strongly acidic — most nutrients unavailable
- pH 4.5–5.5: Strongly acidic — aluminium and manganese toxicity
- pH 5.5–6.5: Moderately acidic — IDEAL for most Kerala crops
- pH 6.5–7.0: Neutral — ideal for pulse crops
- pH > 7.0: Alkaline — micronutrient deficiencies common

KERALA SOIL pH REALITY:
- Most Kerala soils are naturally acidic (pH 4.5–6.0)
- Causes: Heavy rainfall leaches basic cations (Ca, Mg)
- Red laterite soils of Kerala: typically pH 4.8–5.8
- Kuttanad backwaters: pH can go below 4.0 (acid sulphate soils)

LIMING FOR ACID SOILS:
- Agricultural Lime (CaCO3): Apply 1–4 tonnes/ha based on pH
- Dolomitic Lime: Better than agricultural lime — supplies Mg too
- Lime slurry: For paddy fields, mix lime in standing water
- When to apply: 4–6 weeks before planting (not with fertilizers)
- pH correction chart for Kerala:
  * To raise pH from 4.5 to 6.0: Apply 2.5–4 tonnes lime/ha
  * To raise pH from 5.0 to 6.0: Apply 1.5–2.5 tonnes lime/ha
  * To raise pH from 5.5 to 6.5: Apply 0.5–1.5 tonnes lime/ha

SECTION 2: ORGANIC MATTER AND SOIL CARBON
=======================================================================

Organic carbon is the backbone of soil fertility in Kerala.

IMPORTANCE OF ORGANIC MATTER:
- Improves soil structure and water holding capacity
- Feeds soil microorganisms (biological activity)
- Releases nutrients slowly (nutrient buffering)
- Reduces aluminium toxicity in acid soils

ORGANIC MATTER STATUS IN KERALA:
- Desirable: >1.5% organic carbon
- Kerala average: 0.5–1.0% (declining trend)
- Kuttanad: 2–4% (high due to organic sediments)

WAYS TO BUILD ORGANIC MATTER:

1. Farm Yard Manure (FYM):
   - Fully composted FYM: Apply 5–10 tonnes/ha
   - Composition: N 0.5%, P 0.3%, K 0.5%
   - Green FYM (cattle/buffalo dung directly): Only if well-composted

2. Compost Making:
   - Heap method: Layer crop residues, cattle dung, soil alternately
   - Decomposition time: 2–3 months (turn every 3–4 weeks)
   - Add Trichoderma viride to accelerate decomposition
   - Final C:N ratio should be <20:1

3. Green Manuring:
   - Sesbania rostrata (rostrata sesbania): Best for paddy — fixes 40–60 kg N/ha
   - Crotalaria juncea (sunnhemp): For upland crops
   - Mucuna pruriens (velvet bean): For coconut gardens
   - Incorporate green manure at 50% flowering stage

4. Vermicomposting:
   - Use Eisenia fetida (red wiggler earthworms)
   - Feed kitchen waste, crop residues, paper
   - Production: 1 kg worms → 4 kg vermicompost in 45–60 days
   - Vermicompost NPK: 1.5–2.5% N, 1.5–2% P, 1–1.5% K

5. Paddy Straw Incorporation:
   - Do not burn paddy straw — incorporate into soil
   - Apply urea @ 10 kg/tonne of straw to aid decomposition
   - Retains: 0.5% N, 0.2% P, 1.5% K per tonne

SECTION 3: ESSENTIAL PLANT NUTRIENTS
=======================================================================

PRIMARY MACRONUTRIENTS:

NITROGEN (N):
- Role: Leaf and stem growth, protein synthesis, chlorophyll
- Deficiency signs: Yellowing starts from older leaves, V-shaped yellow pattern
- Sources: Urea (46% N), Ammonium sulphate (21% N)
- For rice: Apply in 3 splits (basal 25%, tillering 25%, panicle initiation 50%)

PHOSPHORUS (P):
- Role: Root development, flowering, seed formation
- Deficiency signs: Purple or red coloration of older leaves; poor root growth
- Sources: Single Superphosphate (16% P2O5), Triple Superphosphate (46% P2O5)
- Low mobility in soil — always apply as basal dose

POTASSIUM (K):
- Role: Disease resistance, water regulation, grain filling
- Deficiency signs: Scorching/browning at leaf margins starting from older leaves
- Sources: Muriate of Potash (60% K2O), Sulphate of Potash (50% K2O)

SECONDARY NUTRIENTS:

CALCIUM (Ca):
- Role: Cell wall structure, root tip growth
- In Kerala soils: Often deficient due to leaching
- Management: Regular liming corrects Ca deficiency

MAGNESIUM (Mg):
- Role: Chlorophyll production, enzyme activation
- Deficiency: Interveinal chlorosis on older leaves
- Management: Dolomitic lime (contains Mg), MgSO4 spray 0.5%

SULPHUR (S):
- Deficiency increasing in Kerala due to sulphur-free fertilizers
- Symptoms: Yellowing of young leaves (unlike N deficiency)
- Management: Single Superphosphate (contains S), Ammonium sulphate, Gypsum

MICRONUTRIENTS (trace elements):

ZINC:
- Most common micronutrient deficiency in Kerala rice
- Symptoms: New leaves show rusty-brown blotches; stunted plants
- Management: ZnSO4 × 7H2O @ 25 kg/ha basal; foliar spray 0.5% ZnSO4

BORON:
- Critical for coconut and vegetable crops
- Deficiency: Button shedding in coconut, hollow heart in vegetables
- Management: Borax @ 1 kg/ha; foliar spray 0.1% borax

IRON:
- Deficiency in alkaline and waterlogged soils
- Symptoms: Young leaves turn pale green to yellow (interveinal chlorosis)
- Management: FeSO4 spray 0.5% twice at 10-day intervals

SECTION 4: BIOFERTILIZERS
=======================================================================

Biofertilizers are living microorganisms that improve soil fertility naturally.

RECOMMENDED BIOFERTILIZERS FOR KERALA:

1. Azospirillum (for grasses/cereals):
   - Fixes atmospheric nitrogen (20–30 kg N/ha equivalent)
   - Apply @ 200 g/ha (seed treatment or soil application)
   - Best for rice, sugarcane, maize

2. Phosphate Solubilizing Bacteria (PSB):
   - Releases fixed phosphorus in soil
   - Apply @ 200 g/ha
   - Compatible with chemical phosphorus fertilizers (use together)

3. Rhizobium (for legumes):
   - Fixes N2 in root nodules of legumes
   - Apply as seed coating before sowing cowpea, groundnut, beans
   - Reduces nitrogen fertilizer requirement by 50%

4. Trichoderma viride:
   - Biological fungicide — controls soil-borne diseases
   - Apply @ 4 g/kg seed (seed treatment) or 2.5 kg/ha (soil application)
   - Controls Fusarium, Pythium, Rhizoctonia root rots

5. Mycorrhizal Fungi (VAM):
   - Enhances phosphorus and water uptake
   - Apply during transplanting for vegetables

IMPORTANT: Do not mix biofertilizers with chemical fertilizers or pesticides.
Apply biofertilizers in the evening (sunlight reduces viability).

SECTION 5: SOIL CONSERVATION IN HILLY AREAS (Wayanad/Idukki)
=======================================================================

- Contour farming: Cultivate across slopes, not down the slope
- Bunds and terracing: Essential for slopes >5%
- Vetiver (Chrysopogon zizanioides) grass strips: Plant along contours to prevent erosion
- Cover crops: Mucuna bean or Stylosanthes between plantation rows
- Avoid burning forest areas for cultivation (causes topsoil loss)
