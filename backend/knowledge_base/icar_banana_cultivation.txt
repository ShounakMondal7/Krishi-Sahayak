ICAR TECHNICAL BULLETIN — Banana Cultivation Guide for Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, and Gujarat
Source: ICAR-National Research Centre for Banana (NRCB), Tiruchirappalli, Tamil Nadu
Document Reference: ICAR-NRCB/Tech/2024/AP-TN-MH-GJ-01

India is the world's largest banana producer — 30.5 million tonnes from 8.5 lakh hectares.
Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra (Jalgaon), Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, and Karnataka are leading producers.
Banana is the most consumed fruit in India (consumed by all income groups).

=======================================================================
SECTION 1: RECOMMENDED VARIETIES — STATE AND PURPOSE WISE
=======================================================================

ROBUSTA (William/Grande Naine):
- Most exported variety globally
- Best for: Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra (Jalgaon), Gujarat
- Bunch weight: 25–40 kg | Duration: 12–14 months
- Susceptible to Fusarium wilt (avoid in endemic areas)

GRAND NAINE:
- Taller, hardier than Robusta
- Best for: Maharashtra (Jalgaon) — exports
- Bunch weight: 30–50 kg
- Premium price in Middle East export market

RASTHALI (Silk Banana):
- Most popular dessert banana in Tamil Nadu, Andhra
- Best table variety — aromatic, sweet
- Bunch weight: 10–15 kg | Duration: 12 months
- Highly susceptible to Fusarium wilt

POOVAN (ELAKKI BALE):
- Most widely grown banana in South India
- Hardy, tolerates waterlogging
- Bunch weight: 8–12 kg | Good for rain-fed conditions

NENDRAN:
- MOST IMPORTANT variety for Kerala farmers
- Used for chips, baby food, cooked banana (plantain use)
- Bunch weight: 12–18 kg | High value crop

RED BANANA (Lal Velchi):
- Premium market price (2–3x Robusta)
- Bunch weight: 15–25 kg
- Grown in Jalgaon (Maharashtra) and Tamil Nadu

KARPURAVALLI:
- Good for Andhra Pradesh
- Disease tolerant | Long shelf life

G9 (Cavendish hybrid):
- Tissue culture plants — disease-free start
- Bunch weight: 35–55 kg
- Grown widely in Gujarat's Saurashtra, AP

SECTION 2: PLANTING AND ESTABLISHMENT
=======================================================================

PLANTING MATERIAL:
1. Tissue Culture Plants (TC) — BEST:
   - Disease-free from certified laboratory
   - Uniform growth, early bearing (10–11 months)
   - Higher yield potential (30–40% more than suckers)
   - Cost: Rs 15–20/plant (government subsidies available)
   
2. Sword Suckers (traditional):
   - Select 1–1.5 kg suckers with narrow leaves
   - Treat with Carbendazim 1 g/litre before planting
   
3. Rhizome Bits (for propagation):
   - Less preferred due to disease transmission

PLANTING SEASON:
- South India: June–July (monsoon) or Oct–Nov (second season)
- Maharashtra (Jalgaon): June–July
- Gujarat: June or January–February (winter planting)

SPACING:
- High Density: 1.5 m × 1.5 m (4,444 plants/ha) — Robusta, Grand Naine
- Medium Density: 1.8 m × 1.8 m (3,086 plants/ha)
- Paired Row: (1.5+0.9) m × 1.8 m — allows intercropping

PLANTING DEPTH:
- Pit size: 45 cm × 45 cm × 45 cm
- Fill with topsoil + compost 10 kg + Trichoderma 50 g
- Plant at original ground level (not deeper)

SECTION 3: FERTILIZER MANAGEMENT
=======================================================================

RECOMMENDED DOSE (per plant per year):

| Fertilizer       | Amount    | Application               |
|-----------------|----------|---------------------------|
| FYM/Compost     | 20–25 kg | At planting + 3 months    |
| N (Urea)        | 200 g    | 6 splits over the season  |
| P (SSP)         | 100 g    | 2 splits (at planting +3m)|
| K (MOP)         | 300 g    | 6 splits over the season  |
| Mg (MgSO₄)     | 50 g     | 2 splits                  |
| Boron           | 5 g      | At flowering              |

SPLIT APPLICATION (6 doses):
- 1st month: N 30g + P 50g + K 50g
- 2nd month: N 30g + K 50g
- 3rd month: N 30g + P 50g + K 50g
- 4th month: N 40g + K 50g
- 5th month: N 40g + K 50g + Mg 50g
- 6th month: N 30g + K 50g

FERTIGATION (drip irrigation):
- Apply 25% less fertilizer for same yield
- Daily micro-dosing schedule:
  * 6 g urea + 4 g MOP + 2 g SSP per plant per day (adjust by growth stage)
- Chelated micronutrients (Fe, Zn, Mn, Cu): Monthly spray

POTASSIUM IS CRITICAL:
- Banana is the highest K-consuming crop
- K deficiency: Yellow leaf margins (marginal chlorosis), poor bunch weight
- Apply K in 6–8 splits throughout the crop

SECTION 4: MAJOR DISEASES OF BANANA
=======================================================================

4.1 PANAMA WILT / FUSARIUM WILT (Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. cubense)
MOST DEVASTATING DISEASE — No cure. Can persist in soil for 30+ years.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Lower leaves yellow first, then all leaves yellow and hang down
- Internal brown/red discolouration of rhizome (cut and look)
- Pseudostem shows brown streaks when peeled
- Plants die before bearing fruit

TROPICAL RACE 4 (TR4) — MAJOR THREAT:
- New race attacking Cavendish varieties (Grand Naine, Robusta, G9)
- Present in Gujarat, parts of Maharashtra

MANAGEMENT:
- NO CHEMICAL CURE AVAILABLE
- Use resistant varieties: Poovan, Karpuravalli, BBTV-resistant TC plants
- Sterilize all tools with 1% bleach or 70% alcohol between plants
- Do NOT take soil/plant material from infected fields
- Solarize soil before replanting (polythene sheets for 6 weeks, summer)
- Biological: Trichoderma harzianum 50 g/plant in planting pit
- Do NOT plant Cavendish types in known Panama-endemic areas

4.2 SIGATOKA LEAF SPOT (Mycosphaerella musicola — Yellow Sigatoka)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Pale yellow streaks on lower leaves, expand to brown necrotic patches
- Leaves dry and die from the tips downward
- Reduces photosynthesis and bunch weight significantly

MANAGEMENT:
- Remove and destroy badly affected leaves (leaf pruning)
- Propiconazole 25 EC: 1.5 ml/litre spray on leaves
- Copper oxychloride 50 WP: 3 g/litre (preventive)
- Good drainage and proper spacing reduce severity
- Silicon spray (SiO₂ 0.2%) strengthens leaf wax — reduces infection

4.3 BLACK SIGATOKA (Mycosphaerella fijiensis) — More Severe
- Dark streaks with yellow halo — more aggressive than Yellow Sigatoka
- Present in some parts of Northeast India
- Propiconazole 25 EC at 1.5 ml/litre + oil emulsion

4.4 BANANA BUNCHY TOP VIRUS (BBTV)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Leaves remain small, upright, and bunched at top (like a rosette)
- Dark green streaks/dashes on leaf midrib and petiole ("Morse code" streaks)
- Spread by banana aphid (Pentalonia nigronervosa)
- Infected plant NEVER produces fruit

MANAGEMENT:
- Remove infected plants immediately (including rhizome) — use kerosene injection
- Control aphid vector:
  * Dimethoate 30 EC: 1 ml/litre spray
  * Imidacloprid 17.8 SL: 0.5 ml/litre
- Use BBTV-certified TC plants only
- Inspect new TC plants before purchase

4.5 CROWN ROT (Aspergillus, Botryodiplodia)
POST-HARVEST disease — affects marketability

MANAGEMENT:
- Harvest with 2 cm of stem
- Bavistin (Carbendazim) dip after harvest: 1 g/litre
- Store at 13°C to slow decay

=======================================================================
SECTION 5: MAJOR PESTS
=======================================================================

5.1 BANANA STEM WEEVIL (Odoiporus longicollis)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Adults: Reddish-brown weevil, 20–25 mm, found in pseudostem
- Grubs tunnel inside pseudostem causing rotting
- Oozing red/brown liquid from pseudostem (weevil frass)
- Pseudostem breaks at wind/rain (weak due to internal damage)

MANAGEMENT:
- Remove crop debris after harvest (weevil breeding site)
- Phorate 10G: 2g per pseudostem cut (injected into slit)
- Chlorpyrifos 20 EC: 5 ml/plant (applied to pseudostem base)
- Pheromone traps with Aggregation pheromone (Odoilure): 20 traps/ha
- Biological: Beauveria bassiana 10 g/plant

5.2 THRIPS (Thrips hawaiiensis)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Silver scarring on fruits inside bunch
- Reduce fruit quality and market price

MANAGEMENT:
- Covering bunch with polyethylene bags at shooting stage
- Imidacloprid 17.8 SL: 0.5 ml/litre spray on emerging bunch

5.3 NEMATODES (Radopholus similis, Pratylenchus coffeae)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Brown/black lesions on roots
- Toppling of plants (banana falls over in wind)
- Poor bunch development

MANAGEMENT:
- Use TC plants (nematode-free)
- Carbofuran 3G: 40 g/plant every 6 months in soil
- Paecilomyces lilacinus (bioagent): 10 g/plant

=======================================================================
SECTION 6: BUNCH MANAGEMENT AND HARVEST
=======================================================================

BUNCH CARE:
- Deflowering: Remove flower tips 15 days after shooting (improves fruit size)
- Propping: Bamboo/rope support for heavy bunches (prevents toppling)
- Bunch cover: White polypropylene sleeve — protects from thrips, sunburn, dust
  * Use bags with 1% Imidacloprid treatment for thrips control

HARVEST:
- Harvest at 75–80% maturity (green stage for market)
- Full-size fruits: 3/4 filled (round appearance, green, no angles)
- Premature harvest → poor flavour, shrivelled fruits

RIPENING:
- Ethylene gas treatment: 100 ppm ethylene for 24 hours at 20°C
  (commercially done in ripening chambers)
- Traditional: Calcium carbide 1 g per kg fruit (wrapped in newspaper)
  Note: Calcium carbide ripening is ILLEGAL in some states due to acetylene gas.

MARKET AND PRICES:
- Grand Naine export: Rs 1,200–2,000/quintal
- Nendran (Kerala): Rs 2,500–4,000/quintal
- Rasthali: Rs 1,500–2,500/quintal
