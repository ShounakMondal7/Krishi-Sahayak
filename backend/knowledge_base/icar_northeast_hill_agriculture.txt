ICAR & TEA BOARD TECHNICAL BULLETIN — Northeast India & Hill Agriculture Advisory
Source: ICAR Research Complex for NEH Region, Umiam (Meghalaya) & Tocklai Tea Research Institute, Jorhat (Assam)
Document Reference: ICAR-NEH/Tech/2024/NE-01

The Northeastern Region of India (Assam, Meghalaya, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Manipur,
Mizoram, Tripura) represents a unique agro-climatic biodiversity hotspot with high rainfall (2000–11000 mm),
acidic soils (pH 4.5–5.5), and fragile hill ecosystems. This bulletin provides verified guidelines for
tea, hill horticulture, high-value spices, and sustainable sloping land management.

=======================================================================
SECTION 1: TEA CULTIVATION & MANAGEMENT (Assam, Darjeeling, Tripura)
=======================================================================

1.1 RECOMMENDED TEA CLONES:
- Assam Plains: TV1, TV9, TV20, TV22, TV23, TV25, TV26 (high-yielding vegetative clones)
- Quality clones: TV1, S.3A/1 (Borbhetta), Teen Ali 17/1/54
- Drought/Flood tolerant: TV29, TV30 (resilient to erratic monsoon extremes)
- Darjeeling Hills: AV2, Bannockburn 157, Phoobsering 312 (orthodox high-flavour China hybrids)

1.2 SOIL & PLANTING:
- Soil pH: 4.5 to 5.5 (strictly acidic; lime application if pH < 4.0; never plant if pH > 5.8)
- Organic carbon must be >1.5%; drainage is paramount (tea cannot tolerate waterlogging)
- Spacing: Single hedge: 105 cm × 75 cm (12,698 bushes/ha) or Staggered double hedge: (105+60) cm × 75 cm
- Planting Season: Autumn (October–November) or Spring (March–April with pre-monsoon showers)

1.3 PRUNING & PLUCKING CYCLES:
- Light Prune (LP) every 3–4 years at 45–60 cm height to reset bush architecture
- Skiffing (Deep, Medium, Light) used between prune years to regulate flush
- Plucking Standard: "Two leaves and a bud" (fine plucking ensures premium CTC/orthodox export grade)
- Plucking round: 7–8 days interval in peak monsoon (June–August); 10–12 days in autumn

1.4 MAJOR PESTS & DISEASES:
- Tea Mosquito Bug (Helopeltis theivora):
  * Symptoms: Brown necrotic circular patches on young tender leaves; curled shoots.
  * Management: Thiamethoxam 25 WG @ 100 g/ha or Clothianidin 50 WDG @ 80 g/ha.
  * Organic/Bio: Beauveria bassiana 2 kg/ha or Neem formulation (Azadirachtin 5%) @ 2 ml/L.
- Red Spider Mite (Oligonychus coffeae):
  * Symptoms: Bronzing of mature leaves on upper surface in dry spells (March–May).
  * Management: Hexythiazox 5.45 EC @ 400 ml/ha or Spiromesifen 22.9 SC @ 400 ml/ha.
  * Ensure coverage under leaf surface. Maintain shade trees to reduce mite outbreaks.
- Blister Blight (Exobasidium vexans) — severe in cloudy hill conditions:
  * Symptoms: Translucent pale circular blisters on young leaves; white powdery spore deposits.
  * Management: Copper Oxychloride 50 WP (2.5 g/L) + Hexaconazole 5 EC (1 ml/L) weekly rounds.
- Black Rot (Corticium theae):
  * Management: Prune infected twigs, improve aeration, spray Copper Oxychloride 0.25%.

SECTION 2: HIGH-VALUE SPICES & CROPS OF NORTHEAST INDIA
=======================================================================

2.1 LARGE CARDAMOM (Amomum subulatum) — Sikkim & Arunachal Pradesh:
- Sikkim produces 85% of India's Large Cardamom (Badi Elaichi).
- Varieties: Ramsey (high altitude >1500m), Golsey (mid altitude 1000–1500m), Sawney, Varlangey.
- Planting under shade trees (Alnus nepalensis / Utis is the ideal nitrogen-fixing nurse tree).
- Spacing: 1.5 m × 1.5 m on hill terraces.
- Viral Diseases (Chirke and Foorkey):
  * Chirke causes mosaic streaks; Foorkey causes stunted multiple rosette shoots without flowering.
  * Vectors: Corn aphid (Rhopalosiphum maidis) for Chirke; Banana aphid for Foorkey.
  * Control: Rogue out and burn infected clumps immediately. Spray systemic insecticide on aphids.
- Curing: Traditional Bhatti produces smoky aroma; modified ICAR/Spices Board flue pipe dryers
  produce export-quality green-retained capsules fetching 25% price premium.

2.2 NAGA KING CHILLI / BHUT JOLOKIA (Capsicum chinense):
- GI tagged, SHU rating 855,000–1,041,000 (among the hottest chillies in the world).
- Planting: Seed sowing in nursery Dec–Jan; transplanting Feb–March in valleys, April on hill slopes.
- Soil: Well-drained sandy loam rich in organic matter. Mulching with forest litter essential.
- Major threat: Anthracnose fruit rot & Colletotrichum dieback:
  * Spray Mancozeb 75 WP (2.5 g/L) or Azoxystrobin 23 SC (1 ml/L).
- Aphids & Whitefly control: Spray Neem oil 3 ml/L or Imidacloprid 0.3 ml/L prior to flowering.

2.3 MEGHALAYA LAKADONG TURMERIC:
- World-famous for ultra-high curcumin content (7.0% to 9.2% vs national average 2.5–3.5%).
- Grown in Jaintia Hills, Meghalaya. Organically cultivated using traditional FYM and forest mulch.
- Spacing: 30 cm × 25 cm on raised ridges across the slope.
- Harvest: 9 months after planting when leaves dry. Certified Lakadong commands double market price.

2.4 KHASI MANDARIN (Citrus reticulata) — Meghalaya & Assam:
- Famous GI-tagged loose-jacket orange.
- Citrus Decline Problem (dieback of twigs, yellowing, poor yield):
  * Causes: Phytophthora root rot, Zinc/Boron deficiency, Citrus Tristeza Virus (CTV).
  * Management: Apply Trichoderma viride enriched FYM (10 kg/tree).
  * Spray Bordeaux mixture 1% before monsoon and post-monsoon on trunk and canopy.
  * Micronutrient spray: Zinc sulphate (0.5%) + Copper sulphate (0.2%) + Boric acid (0.1%).

=======================================================================
SECTION 3: SOIL CONSERVATION & SHIFTING CULTIVATION REHABILITATION
=======================================================================

3.1 SLOPE MANAGEMENT & TERRACING:
- On slopes >20%, bench terracing and contour bunding reduce topsoil erosion by up to 85%.
- Contour hedgerows of Flemingia macrophylla or Tephrosia candida fix nitrogen and trap sediment.
- Mulching: Thick biomass mulch (paddy straw / hill grass) suppresses weeds and prevents rain splash.

3.2 JHUM (SLASH AND BURN) REHABILITATION:
- Traditional 15–20 year jhum cycle has reduced to 3–5 years, causing severe soil erosion and degradation.
- ICAR Alternative Model: Three-tier watershed agroforestry system:
  * Top ridge: Forestry/timber species (Alnus, Michelia) for soil & water recharge.
  * Mid slope: Horticultural fruit trees (Khasi Mandarin, Assam Lemon, Guava, Kiwi).
  * Lower terrace: Terraced arable crops (paddy, maize, pulses, vegetables) with livestock integration.

=======================================================================
SECTION 4: COLD-TOLERANT HIGH-ALTITUDE PADDY
=======================================================================
- Varieties for valley and mid-hills: RC Mani Phou, Megha Rice 1, Megha Rice 2, Bhalum 1, Bhalum 2 (upland).
- High altitude cold-tolerant: NEH Mega Rice 1 (tolerates low temperature during flowering).
- Organic nutrient protocol: Sesbania green manuring + Azolla dual cropping in wet terraces.
