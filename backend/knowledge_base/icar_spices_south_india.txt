ICAR TECHNICAL BULLETIN — Spices Cultivation Advisory: Pepper, Cardamom, Turmeric, and Ginger for South India
Source: ICAR-Indian Institute of Spices Research (IISR), Kozhikode, Kerala
Document Reference: ICAR-IISR/Tech/2024/IN-SP-01

India is the world's largest producer and exporter of spices, earning Rs 25,000 crore in foreign exchange.
The "Spice Garden of India" states: Kerala, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, and Andhra Pradesh together produce
95% of India's major spices. This bulletin covers black pepper, cardamom, turmeric, and ginger.

=======================================================================
SECTION 1: BLACK PEPPER (Piper nigrum)
=======================================================================

World's most traded spice. India produces 70,000–80,000 tonnes/year.
Kerala accounts for 96% of India's pepper production.

1.1 VARIETIES
1. Panniyur 1: Most popular hybrid (♀ Cheriyakaniyakadan × ♂ Uthirankotta)
   - Yield: 2–3 kg dry pepper/vine/year
   - Early bearing (3rd year) | Tolerates waterlogging
   
2. Pournami: High yielding (3.5–4 kg/vine) | Multiple spike-bearing
3. Sreekara: Suitable for homestead gardens | Phytophthora tolerant
4. Subhakara: High yielding | Less Phytophthora susceptible
5. Karimunda (Heritage variety): Most preferred quality pepper globally
   - Lower yield (0.5–1 kg/vine) but premium price
   - Oleoresin content: 14% (highest quality)
   
6. IISR Thevam: Released for Wayanad | Phytophthora tolerant

1.2 ESTABLISHMENT
- Stem cuttings (runner shoots): 2–3 node cuttings
- Nursery: Poly bags with river sand + FYM (1:1) for 3–4 months
- Standard: 60–120 cm rooted cuttings for field planting
- Planting season: June–July (SW monsoon)
- Spacing: 3 m × 3 m (standard) along live/dead standards
- Standards (support trees):
  * Garuga pinnata (Kula mara) — most preferred
  * Erythrina indica (Murukku)
  * Dead standards (concrete/GI poles)

TRAINING:
- Tie runner shoots to standard every 4–6 weeks
- Remove unwanted lateral (flag) shoots to promote vertical growth
- Top budding at 5–6 m height

1.3 FERTILIZER (per vine per year, full bearing):
- FYM: 10 kg (June — pre-monsoon)
- N: 100 g (urea: 220 g) | Applied June + August
- P₂O₅: 40 g | Applied June (full dose)
- K₂O: 140 g (MOP: 233 g) | Applied June + September

MICRONUTRIENTS (Critical for yield):
- Magnesium sulphate: 500 g/vine/year (soil application)
- Calcium: Lime 500 g/vine (apply February)
- Zinc: ZnSO₄ 5 g/vine (spray 0.5% on leaves)
- Micronutrient cocktail spray (spray 3 times/year):
  ZnSO₄ + FeSO₄ + MgSO₄ + Borax (mixed)

1.4 DISEASES OF BLACK PEPPER

PHYTOPHTHORA FOOT ROT (Quick Wilt) — Phytophthora capsici:
THE MOST DESTRUCTIVE DISEASE. Can kill a vine in 2–3 weeks.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Sudden wilting of the entire vine during monsoon
- Dark water-soaked lesion at collar region (base of stem)
- Rapid leaf shedding
- Internal stem tissue becomes dark brown on cutting

MANAGEMENT:
- Preventive soil drench: Metalaxyl-M + Mancozeb (2.5 g/litre) at base of vine — EVERY YEAR before monsoon (May)
- Curative: Potassium phosphonate (Aliette): 5 ml/litre spray + soil drench
- Bordeaux Mixture 1%: 3 litres per vine (soil drench + spray, June and August)
- Remove and destroy infected vines completely (don't carry to nursery)
- Improve drainage around standards
- Avoid wounding roots during weeding

POLLU DISEASE (Colletotrichum gloeosporioides) — Anthracnose:
IDENTIFICATION:
- Small black dots on berries — "shot-hole" appearance
- Premature berry drop (pollu = hollow/empty berry)
- 20–40% yield loss in epidemic years

MANAGEMENT:
- Bordeaux Mixture 1%: Spray 3 times from June at 30-day intervals
- Carbendazim 50 WP: 1 g/litre spray

SLOW DECLINE (Radopholus similis — nematode):
IDENTIFICATION:
- Gradual yellowing, loss of leaves over months
- Poor new shoot production
- Roots show brown lesions (nematode damage)

MANAGEMENT:
- Carbofuran 3G: 40 g/vine every 6 months
- Paecilomyces lilacinus (bioagent): 10 g/vine

1.5 PESTS OF BLACK PEPPER

POLLU BEETLE (Longitarsus nigripennis):
- Tiny black beetles; larvae feed inside developing berries
- Infested berries are empty (pollu)
MANAGEMENT:
- Dimethoate 30 EC: 2 ml/litre spray (June + August)
- Pyrethroids also effective

TOP SHOOT BORER (Cydia hemidoxa):
- Bore into growing tip and young spikes
MANAGEMENT: Monocrotophos 36 SL 1.5 ml/litre

PRICES (2024):
- Black pepper (dry, MG1): Rs 700–900/kg
- Export price: $7–12/kg (USD)

=======================================================================
SECTION 2: CARDAMOM (Elettaria cardamomum)
=======================================================================

"Queen of Spices." India (mainly Idukki district, Kerala) and Guatemala are the
major producers. Cardamom is the third most expensive spice in the world.

2.1 VARIETIES
1. IISR Avinash (PV-1): Yield 150–200 kg/ha dry | Clump forming
2. IISR Suvasini (CL-759): Good capsule size, strong aroma
3. Mysore (Type IV): Traditional Kerala variety | Best aroma
4. Vazhukka: Erect variety | High yielding for open conditions
5. Njallani (Green Gold): Premium, large capsule | High market price

2.2 ESTABLISHMENT
- Planting material: Suckers or seedlings (12–18 months old)
- Planting season: June–August (SW monsoon)
- Spacing: 3 m × 3 m (conventional) or 2 m × 2 m (intensive)
- Shade requirement: 40–50% shade (under forest or artificial shade)
- Temperature: 15–35°C | Altitude: 600–1500 m (ideal: 900–1200 m)
- Soil: Rich forest loam, well-drained, slightly acidic pH 5.0–6.5

2.3 FERTILIZER (per hectare per year):
- FYM: 5 tonnes
- N: 75 kg | P₂O₅: 75 kg | K₂O: 150 kg
- Applied in 2 splits: June and September
- Micronutrients: Foliar spray of ZnSO₄ 0.2% + MgSO₄ 1% at 2-monthly intervals

2.4 DISEASES

KATTE (Cardamom Mosaic):
Most serious disease — caused by Cardamom Mosaic Virus (CdMV), spread by aphids.
IDENTIFICATION:
- Mosaic chlorosis on young leaves
- Yellow and green streaks running along leaf veins
- Severely reduced yield; plant gradually declines

MANAGEMENT:
- No cure — remove infected clumps immediately
- Control aphid vector: Dimethoate 30 EC 2 ml/litre
- Use virus-free planting material from certified nurseries
- Rogue out infected plants before aphid season (Nov–Feb)

RHIZOME ROT (Pythium vexans):
- Rhizome becomes brown and soft
MANAGEMENT:
- Soil drench with Metalaxyl 0.4% before monsoon

AZHUKAL (Capsule rot — Phytophthora meadii):
- Capsules show water-soaked patches, turn brown
- Heavy yield loss in rainy season
MANAGEMENT:
- Copper oxychloride 0.3% spray on capsule clusters (3 rounds)

2.5 PESTS

THRIPS (Sciothrips cardamomi):
- Causes "chenthal" — brown scarring on capsules
- Significant quality reduction
MANAGEMENT:
- Dimethoate 30 EC 2 ml/litre spray

SHOOT BORER:
- Bores into young shoots
MANAGEMENT: Chlorpyrifos 20 EC 2 ml/litre

PRICES:
- Cardamom (Indian) auction: Rs 1,200–2,000/kg
- Guatemala (competition) price: $5–7/kg USD

SECTION 3: TURMERIC (Curcuma longa)
=======================================================================

India produces 80% of world's turmeric. Erode (Tamil Nadu) is the world's largest
turmeric trading centre. Telangana (Nizamabad), Maharashtra (Sangli), Andhra Pradesh,
Karnataka, and Odisha are major producers.

3.1 VARIETIES
1. Erode Local (TDN 82): Tamil Nadu | Curcumin: 3.5%
2. Alleppey Finger (Kerala): Best for oleoresin | Curcumin: 4.5%
3. Rajendra Sonia (Odisha): Yield 35–40 t/ha fresh
4. Suguna (IISR): High curcumin (5.5%) | Disease tolerant
5. Pragati (IISR): High yielding | Rhizome rot tolerant
6. Nizamabad Bulb (Telangana): GI Tagged | Low curcumin, pale colour

3.2 PLANTING
- Season: May–June (with monsoon)
- Planting material: Mother rhizomes or fingers (40–50 g each)
- Seed rate: 2,000–2,500 kg/ha
- Spacing: 45 cm × 30 cm | Beds 1 m wide, 15 cm high
- Mulching: Paddy straw mulch 10 t/ha at planting (critical)

3.3 FERTILIZER:
- FYM/Compost: 30–40 t/ha (most important)
- N: 80 kg/ha (3 splits: at planting, 45 DAS, 90 DAS)
- P₂O₅: 50 kg/ha (basal)
- K₂O: 80 kg/ha (2 splits)

3.4 DISEASES

RHIZOME ROT (Pythium aphanidermatum):
MOST SERIOUS — can destroy entire crop in heavy-rainfall areas.
IDENTIFICATION:
- Young shoots wilt and collapse at base
- Rhizome becomes soft and emits foul smell
MANAGEMENT:
- Seed treatment: Metalaxyl 2 g/kg rhizome soak (30 minutes)
- Soil drench: Metalaxyl 0.4% (2 litres/plant at planting)
- Raise beds for good drainage

LEAF BLOTCH (Taphrina maculans):
- Brown oval spots with yellow halo
MANAGEMENT: Mancozeb 0.2% spray

3.5 HARVEST
- Harvest when leaves turn yellow and dry (7–9 months)
- Yield: 15–25 t/ha fresh rhizome
- Processing: Boil in water for 45–60 minutes + dry (8–10% moisture)
- Yield of dry turmeric: 15–25% of fresh weight
- MSP 2024: Rs 70–90/kg dry

=======================================================================
SECTION 4: GINGER (Zingiber officinale)
=======================================================================

4.1 VARIETIES
1. Rio-de-Janeiro (Assam / Meghalaya): High oleoresin
2. Maran (Kerala): Bold, good for export
3. Himachal: Less fibre, mild pungency
4. Varada (IISR): Rhizome rot tolerant | High yield
5. IISR Mahima: Very high yielding (25 t/ha)
6. Rejatha (IISR): Export-oriented, less fibre

4.2 PLANTING
- Season: April–May (2–3 weeks before monsoon)
- Planting material: Healthy rhizomes (20–25 g seed pieces)
- Spacing: 30 cm × 25 cm
- Seed rate: 1,500–2,000 kg/ha
- Shade: 25–50% shade improves quality; not required for rain-fed crop

4.3 SOFT ROT (Pythium aphanidermatum) — MOST SERIOUS:
MANAGEMENT: Same as turmeric rhizome rot
- Rhizome treatment: Metalaxyl 2 g/litre + Copper oxychloride 2 g/litre

4.4 HARVEST: 8–10 months (mature ginger, dry ginger) or 5–6 months (green ginger)
- MSP: No official MSP; farm gate price Rs 20–60/kg (highly variable)
