ICAR TECHNICAL BULLETIN — Coconut Cultivation and Pest Management in Kerala
Source: ICAR-Central Plantation Crops Research Institute (CPCRI), Kasaragod, Kerala
Document Reference: ICAR-CPCRI/Tech/2023/KL-05

Kerala accounts for 45% of India's total coconut production. The state has approximately 
7.7 lakh hectares under coconut cultivation, making it the backbone of Kerala's agricultural economy.

=======================================================================
SECTION 1: COCONUT VARIETIES FOR KERALA
=======================================================================

TALL VARIETIES:
1. West Coast Tall (WCT):
   - Most widely grown variety in Kerala
   - Yield: 80–100 nuts/palm/year
   - Copra content: 165–170 g/nut
   - Drought tolerant, suitable for all Kerala districts
   - First bearing: 6–8 years after planting

2. Lakshadweep Ordinary (LCT):
   - Suitable for Lakshadweep and coastal Kerala
   - Yield: 70–80 nuts/palm/year
   - Salt tolerant

HYBRID VARIETIES (High Yielding):
1. Chandrasankara (WCT × Chowghat Orange Dwarf):
   - Most popular hybrid in Kerala
   - Yield: 130–180 nuts/palm/year
   - First bearing: 3–4 years after planting
   - Suitable for: Thrissur, Palakkad, Ernakulam

2. Kera Sankara (WCT × Malayan Yellow Dwarf):
   - Yield: 100–120 nuts/palm/year
   - Good disease tolerance
   - Recommended for Wayanad and Kozhikode

3. Kalpa Raksha:
   - Specifically bred for root wilt disease tolerance
   - Suitable for root wilt endemic areas (Thiruvananthapuram, Kollam)
   - Yield: 80–100 nuts/palm/year

4. Kalpa Mitra (WCT × GBGD):
   - High copra content (200–220 g/nut)
   - Drought tolerant
   - Good for Palakkad dry zone

DWARF VARIETIES:
1. Chowghat Green Dwarf (CGD):
   - Early bearing: 2–3 years
   - Used for tender coconut production
   - Yield: 80–100 green nuts/year
   - Suitable for kitchen gardens

SECTION 2: PLANTING AND MANAGEMENT
=======================================================================

PLANTING SEASON:
- Best: June–September (onset of Southwest monsoon)
- Can also plant December–January if irrigation is available

PIT PREPARATION:
- Pit size: 1 m × 1 m × 1 m
- Fill with topsoil + 50 kg FYM/compost + 1 kg rock phosphate
- Allow pit to weather for 2–4 weeks before planting

SPACING:
- Square system: 7.5 m × 7.5 m (177 palms/ha)
- Triangular system: 8 m × 8 m (160 palms/ha) — better light interception
- For high-density planting: 6 m × 6 m (with intercropping)

IRRIGATION:
- Young palms (0–3 years): 40–45 litres/palm every 3–4 days in summer
- Bearing palms: 200 litres/palm/week in summer
- Drip irrigation: 12–15 litres/day (best practice, saves 50% water)
- Critical period: February–May (pre-monsoon summer)

INTERCROPPING (recommended for income diversification):
- Banana: 2 rows between coconut rows (first 3 years)
- Pineapple: Excellent ground cover under coconut
- Cacao: Shade-tolerant, grows well under coconut canopy
- Pepper: Trained on coconut trunk (living standard)
- Elephant foot yam: High value, shade tolerant
- Black pepper + banana + cacao combination: Most profitable

SECTION 3: FERTILIZER MANAGEMENT
=======================================================================

FERTILIZER RECOMMENDATION FOR BEARING PALMS (per palm per year):

| Stage          | N (Urea)  | P (SSP)    | K (MOP)    | FYM    |
|---------------|-----------|------------|------------|--------|
| 1–3 years     | 130 g     | 200 g      | 200 g      | 20 kg  |
| 4–6 years     | 330 g     | 500 g      | 800 g      | 25 kg  |
| 7+ years (full| 660 g     | 1000 g     | 2000 g     | 25 kg  |
| bearing)      | (= 0.6 kg N)| (= 0.4 kg P₂O₅)| (= 1.2 kg K₂O) | |

SPLIT APPLICATION:
- Apply in 2 equal splits: June (pre-monsoon onset) and December
- Apply in 30–50 cm deep trenches around the drip circle (1.5–2 m from trunk)
- Cover with soil and irrigate

MICRONUTRIENT APPLICATION:
- Borax: 50 g/palm/year — prevents button shedding and improves nut set
  * Apply in June along with main fertilizer
- Magnesium sulphate: 500 g/palm/year — if Mg deficiency observed
- Zinc sulphate: 50 g/palm/year — for Palakkad and sandy soils

ORGANIC SOURCES:
- Coir pith compost: 25 kg/palm — excellent for water retention in sandy soils
- Neem cake: 1 kg/palm — suppresses soil pests (rhinoceros beetle grubs)
- Wood ash: 2 kg/palm — good potassium source for homestead gardens

SECTION 4: MAJOR PESTS AND MANAGEMENT
=======================================================================

4.1 RHINOCEROS BEETLE (Oryctes rhinoceros)
The most destructive pest of coconut in Kerala.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Large black beetle (40–50 mm length) with curved horn
- Adults bore into the base of unopened spear leaves
- Characteristic V-shaped or diamond-shaped cuts on newly opened leaves
- Tunnelling into growing point can kill the palm

LIFE CYCLE:
- Eggs laid in decaying organic matter, compost heaps, dead palm trunks
- Larva stage: 3–6 months (white C-shaped grubs in organic matter)
- Pupa: 3–4 weeks underground
- Adult: 4–8 months (can fly up to 5 km)

MANAGEMENT:
1. Cultural Control (most important — prevent breeding):
   - Remove all dead coconut trunks and decaying organic matter
   - Compost heaps should be properly covered or treated
   - Fill hollow stumps with soil or concrete
   - Wrap young palms with dried leaves to protect crown

2. Biological Control (most sustainable):
   - Baculovirus oryctes: Spray/apply virus formulation into crown holes
   - Metarhizium anisopliae (green muscardine fungus): Apply granules (4g) into crown
   - These biocontrol agents multiply within the beetle population

3. Pheromone Traps:
   - Use Oryctalure pheromone traps (1 trap/hectare)
   - Replace lure every 60 days
   - Best for mass trapping during active season (June–September)

4. Chemical Control (only when infestation is severe):
   - Pour 1% Carbaryl solution (10 g/litre) into crown holes
   - Naphthalene balls (5–6 numbers) tied in old gunny bag in crown — repels adults

4.2 RED PALM WEEVIL (Rhynchophorus ferrugineus)
A lethal pest — infected palms rarely survive if treatment is delayed.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Adult: Reddish-brown weevil, 25–40 mm with elongated snout
- External signs: Oozing dark brown, foul-smelling liquid from crown or trunk
- Chewed fibres and frass at the base of crown leaves
- Crown collapse or sudden tilting of the palm top
- Yellowing and drooping of outer leaves before crown collapse

MANAGEMENT:
1. Pheromone Trapping (for early detection and mass trapping):
   - Ferrolure+ pheromone: 1 trap/hectare
   - Install traps at 1.2 m height from ground
   - Bait with sugarcane pieces or pineapple (enhances attraction)
   - Replace lure every 60 days

2. Chemical Treatment (trunk injection for early-stage infection):
   - Monocrotophos 36 WSC: 20 ml in 200 ml water — inject into trunk
   - Chlorpyrifos 20 EC: 10 ml in 500 ml water — inject into bore holes
   - Close holes with clay after injection
   
3. Preventive Spraying:
   - Chlorpyrifos 0.1%: spray on crown and upper trunk monthly
   - Best done from June to October

4. Biological Control:
   - Entomopathogenic nematodes (Steinernema carpocapsae): Pour into bore holes
   - Beauveria bassiana: 5 g/litre spray on trunk

5. Severely Infected Palms:
   - Remove and destroy (burn on site) — do not transport
   - Treat stump with lime to prevent spread

4.3 COCONUT LEAF BEETLE (Brontispa longissima)

IDENTIFICATION:
- Adult: Small, flat, yellowish-brown beetle (8–10 mm)
- Both adults and larvae feed inside unopened (spear) leaves
- Necrotic brownish streaks on leaves once they unfurl
- Severe infestation: all leaves show brown-streaked or dried appearance

MANAGEMENT:
- Apply Dimethoate 30 EC (2 ml/litre) + water into spear leaf
- Biological control: Release Asecodes hispinarum (parasitoid wasp) — available from CPCRI

4.4 ERIOPHYID MITE (Aceria guerreronis) — Coconut Mite

IDENTIFICATION:
- Microscopic mites (not visible to naked eye)
- White triangular patches at the base of nut (perianth area)
- Nuts become deformed, triangular, and smaller in size (button shedding if severe)

MANAGEMENT:
- Lime sulphur spray 0.4% (4 ml/litre) + wetting agent on bunches
- Dicofol 18.5 EC: 5 ml/litre spray on bunches
- Neem oil 3%: 3 ml/litre + 1 ml Teepol (most eco-friendly option)

SECTION 5: DISEASES OF COCONUT
=======================================================================

5.1 ROOT WILT DISEASE (Phytoplasma suspected)
The most serious disease in Kerala — no complete cure available.

Endemic in: Thiruvananthapuram, Kollam, Alappuzha, Ernakulam, Kottayam

IDENTIFICATION:
- Yellowing of lower leaves progressing upward
- Leaflets fold and droop (inverted V-shape on individual leaflets)
- Premature nut fall (button shedding)
- Roots become discoloured and rotten
- Eventual death of palm over 3–15 years

MANAGEMENT (palliative only):
- Remove affected palms to prevent spread (rogueing)
- Fertilize healthy palms to increase resistance
- Grow tolerant varieties: Kalpa Raksha, Kalpa Mitra
- Apply micro-irrigation to reduce water stress
- Spray Monocrotophos 36 WSC (1.5 ml/litre) to control leafhopper vectors (Proutista moesta)

5.2 STEM BLEEDING (Thielaviopsis paradoxa)

IDENTIFICATION:
- Dark reddish-brown liquid oozing from cracks on the trunk (not from crown like RPW)
- Tissue under exudate is brown and necrotic
- Internal trunk cavity in severe cases

MANAGEMENT:
- Chisel out infected tissue and apply Bordeaux paste (1:1:10) to wound
- Drench soil with Copper oxychloride 0.2% solution
- Avoid mechanical injury to trunk

5.3 BUD ROT (Phytophthora palmivora)

IDENTIFICATION:
- Rotting of central bud (spear leaf) with foul smell
- Spear leaf pulls out easily
- Affects mostly young palms and post-cyclone conditions

MANAGEMENT:
- Remove and destroy affected spear leaf and rotted tissue
- Apply Bordeaux mixture 1% in the crown
- Metalaxyl + Mancozeb 72 WP: 3 g/litre spray in crown

=======================================================================
SECTION 6: COCONUT HARVEST AND PROCESSING
=======================================================================

HARVESTING:
- Full nuts for copra/oil: Harvest every 45 days (12 months maturity)
- Green tender coconuts: Harvest at 7 months (water stage)
- Climber safety: Use coconut tree climber or mechanical climber
- Do not allow nuts to fall to ground — reduces copra quality

COPRA PROCESSING:
- Split nut in half using coconut splitter
- Sun-dry for 3–4 days (moisture content < 6%)
- Or kiln-dry: 60°C for 48 hours

COCONUT VALUE-ADDED PRODUCTS (for extra income):
- Virgin Coconut Oil (VCO): High value, health food market
- Coir pith compost: From coconut husks
- Shell charcoal: High-value biomass fuel
- Neera (palm nectar): Collected from inflorescence — high market value
