CROP-WISE ADVISORY FOR KERALA FARMERS — KHARIF 2024
=======================================================================

Zone 1 (High Rainfall): Idukki, Wayanad, Kozhikode uplands — >3000 mm/year
Zone 2 (Moderate Rainfall): Thrissur, Palakkad, Ernakulam — 2000–3000 mm/year
Zone 3 (Low Rainfall): Palakkad Gap, Northern plains — 1500–2000 mm/year

SECTION 1: PADDY (RICE) CULTIVATION
=======================================================================

VIRIPPU SEASON (Kharif — June to September):

Suitable Varieties by District:
- Palakkad: Jyothi (120 days), MO-16 (130 days), Uma (115 days)
- Thrissur: Kanchana (130 days), Gayathri (120 days)
- Alappuzha (Kuttanad): Pokkali (salt-tolerant, 130 days), Njavara (heritage, 90 days)
- Wayanad: Gandhakasala (aromatic, 135 days), Jeerakasala (aromatic, 120 days)
- Ernakulam: Jyothi, Uma, Kanchana

Nursery Management:
- Soak seeds in water for 24 hours, then incubate for 24 hours before sowing
- Nursery rate: 25 kg seeds/ha (puddled bed) or 60 kg/ha (dry bed)
- Sowing time: 1st–15th June for Virippu

Transplanting Guidelines:
- Transplant 25–30 day old seedlings (before 35 days)
- Spacing: 20 cm × 15 cm for medium-duration varieties
- Spacing: 25 cm × 20 cm for long-duration varieties
- Transplant 2–3 seedlings per hill

Fertilizer Recommendation (kg/ha):
- Jyothi/Uma: N:P:K = 70:35:35 kg/ha
- MO-16/Kanchana: N:P:K = 90:45:45 kg/ha
- Apply basal dose (P+K + 25% N) at transplanting
- First top dressing (25% N): 15 days after transplanting
- Second top dressing (50% N): 40 days after transplanting (panicle initiation)

PUNCHA SEASON (Rabi — November to February):
- Shorter duration varieties preferred: Uma (115 days), Jyothi (120 days)
- Reduce nitrogen by 20% due to lower solar radiation

MINIMUM SUPPORT PRICE (MSP) 2024-25:
- Common Paddy: Rs. 2,300 per quintal
- Grade A Paddy: Rs. 2,320 per quintal
- Procurement through: Supplyco, Kerala State Civil Supplies Corporation

SECTION 2: COCONUT CULTIVATION ADVISORY
=======================================================================

Kerala has 54% of India's coconut area. Coconut is the state's primary plantation crop.

FERTILIZER SCHEDULE FOR BEARING PALMS (>4 years):
Apply per palm per year:
- Urea: 1.3 kg (= 0.6 kg N)
- Super phosphate: 2.0 kg (= 0.4 kg P2O5)
- Muriate of Potash: 2.0 kg (= 1.2 kg K2O)
- Lime: 0.5 kg (if pH < 5.5)
- FYM/Compost: 25 kg

Application Time:
- Apply in two equal splits: June and December
- Apply in 30 cm deep trenches around drip circle
- Water the palms after applying fertilizer

COMMON COCONUT PROBLEMS:

1. Button Shedding (premature nut fall):
   - Cause: Water stress, boron deficiency, or pollination failure
   - Management: Irrigate regularly, apply borax 50 g/palm in June

2. Root Wilt Disease:
   - Cause: Phytoplasma (suspected)
   - Symptoms: Yellowing of lower leaves, premature nut fall, stunted growth
   - No cure — manage symptomatically
   - Management: Remove infected palms, apply fertilizers to improve tree vigour, spray Monocrotophos to control leafhopper vectors

3. Rhinoceros Beetle:
   - Symptoms: V-shaped cuts on spear leaves, tunnelling into growing point
   - Management:
     * Remove and destroy breeding sites (dead logs, compost heaps)
     * Pour Monocrotophos 1% solution into crown holes
     * Apply Beauveria bassiana (green muscardine fungus) granules into crown
     * Pheromone traps with Oryctalure (1 trap/ha)

4. Red Palm Weevil:
   - Symptoms: Oozing of dark brown fluid with fermenting smell from crown, crown collapse
   - Management:
     * Trunk injection with Monocrotophos 36 WSC (20 ml diluted in 200 ml water)
     * Pheromone traps (Ferrolure) — most effective monitoring tool
     * Fill trunk holes with tar

5. Lethal Yellowing:
   - Spray Oxytetracycline hydrochloride as trunk injection

COCONUT HYBRID VARIETIES RECOMMENDED FOR KERALA:
- Chandrasankara: High yielding (150 nuts/palm/year), drought tolerant
- Kera Sankara: Disease tolerant, 130 nuts/palm/year
- Kalpa Raksha: Tolerant to root wilt disease

SECTION 3: VEGETABLE CULTIVATION ADVISORY
=======================================================================

KITCHEN GARDEN VEGETABLES — SEASON-WISE GUIDE:

SUMMER (February–May):
Recommended: Bittergourd (Pavakka), Ridgegourd (Peechinga), Snakegourd, Ash gourd
- Sow seeds directly in raised beds (1 m wide, 30 cm high)
- Spacing: 2 m × 2 m for climbing vegetables
- Irrigation: 1–2 times daily during summer

MONSOON (June–September):
- Avoid waterlogging — use raised beds (60–90 cm height)
- Recommended: Amaranthus, Cowpea, Yardlong beans
- Apply lime @ 200 g/sq.m to reduce fungal diseases
- Raised beds essential for all cucurbits

WINTER (October–January):
- Ideal season for tomato, brinjal, capsicum, leafy vegetables
- Best quality tomatoes produced in this season

FERTILIZER FOR VEGETABLES (per 10 square metre plot):
- FYM: 25 kg + Urea: 75 g + Super phosphate: 200 g + MOP: 75 g

POPULAR VEGETABLE VARIETIES:
- Bittergourd: Preethi, Priya (bitter, high yield)
- Cowpea: Lola, Bhaskar, GC-3
- Amaranthus: CO-1, Arun
- Tomato: Sakthi, Mukthi, Arka Vikas

SECTION 4: SOIL HEALTH MANAGEMENT
=======================================================================

SOIL TESTING:
- Every farmer should test soil at least once in 3 years
- Contact nearest Soil Testing Laboratory (STL) — fee: Rs. 20-50/sample
- Test for pH, organic carbon, N, P, K, micronutrients

SOIL pH GUIDELINES:
- Rice: 5.5–6.5 (slightly acidic)
- Coconut: 5.5–7.0
- Vegetables: 6.0–7.0
- If pH < 5.5: Apply agricultural lime or dolomite

ORGANIC CARBON IMPROVEMENT:
- Apply FYM/compost: 5–10 tonnes/ha annually
- Green manuring with Sesbania or Crotalaria
- Incorporation of crop residues

MICRONUTRIENT DEFICIENCIES IN KERALA:
1. Zinc Deficiency:
   - Symptoms: Young leaves become pale yellow with brown spots
   - Management: Spray ZnSO4 0.5% (5 g/litre) or basal application of ZnSO4 @ 25 kg/ha

2. Boron Deficiency:
   - Common in coconut — causes button shedding
   - Management: Apply borax 50 g/palm or foliar spray 0.1% borax solution

3. Iron Deficiency:
   - Yellowing of younger leaves while older leaves remain green
   - Management: FeSO4 spray 0.5% twice at 10-day intervals

SECTION 5: IRRIGATION ADVISORY
=======================================================================

RICE WATER MANAGEMENT:
- Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD): 
  * Saves 30% water without yield loss
  * Allow water to drop 15 cm below soil surface, then re-irrigate
  * Stop AWD 2 weeks before harvest

- Flood irrigation not required throughout season
- Critical water requirement periods:
  * Tillering stage
  * Panicle initiation (flowering)
  * Grain filling (milky stage)

DRIP IRRIGATION FOR VEGETABLES:
- Subsidy available: 55–75% under PMKSY scheme
- Contact District Agriculture Office for application
