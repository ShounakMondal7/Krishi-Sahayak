ICAR TECHNICAL BULLETIN — Organic Farming and Natural Farming Practices for Indian Farmers
Source: ICAR-National Centre of Organic and Natural Farming (NCONF), Ghaziabad, Uttar Pradesh
Document Reference: ICAR-NCONF/Tech/2024/IN-01

India has over 44 lakh organic certified farmers — the largest number in the world.
Organic farming area is 59.12 lakh hectares (2022-23). Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Maharashtra,
Gujarat, and Andhra Pradesh (Natural Farming/ZBNF) are leaders.
Organic premium: 20–50% higher price in domestic and export markets.

=======================================================================
SECTION 1: WHY ORGANIC FARMING?
=======================================================================

ECONOMIC BENEFITS:
- Organic premium: 20–50% higher price for certified produce
- Lower input cost (no chemical fertilizer/pesticide after 3-year transition)
- Export market: Growing Rs 15,000 crore organic export from India
- PGS-India certification: Free organic certification for small farmers

SOIL HEALTH BENEFITS:
- Increases soil organic carbon (critical for water retention)
- Improves beneficial soil microbial population
- Reduces soil compaction over time

CHALLENGES:
- 2–3 year transition period (yield drops during transition)
- Organic certification process takes time
- Higher labour input during transition

SECTION 2: SOIL FERTILITY MANAGEMENT IN ORGANIC FARMING
=======================================================================

2.1 COMPOST PREPARATION

NADEP COMPOST (Narayan Deotao Pandhari Pandhre):
Best compost for Indian conditions. Ready in 90 days.

MATERIALS:
- Agricultural waste (crop residues, dry leaves): 1,500 kg
- Farmyard manure (cattle dung): 100 kg
- Soil: 1,750 kg
- Water: 1,500 litres

CONSTRUCTION:
- Brick tank: 12 ft × 5.5 ft × 3 ft (length × width × height)
- Layer 1: Agricultural waste 6 inch thick
- Layer 2: Cow dung slurry spread on waste
- Layer 3: Topsoil 6 inch thick
- Repeat layers until full
- Plaster outside with mud (to prevent drying)
- Ready in 90 days — C:N ratio drops from 70:1 to 18:1

VERMICOMPOST:
- Earthworms: Eisenia fetida (red wigglers) — most efficient
- Ratio: 3 kg worm per m² bed area
- Feed: Any organic waste (cow dung, kitchen waste, crop residues)
- Ready in 60–90 days
- Quality: NPK 2.5:1.5:1.5 (higher than normal compost)
- Application: 2.5–5 t/ha for field crops

2.2 PANCHAGAVYA (Traditional liquid biofertilizer):
The most effective organic growth promoter from Vedic tradition.

PREPARATION (200 litres final volume):
- Cow dung (fresh): 5 kg
- Cow urine: 3 litres
- Cow's milk: 2 litres
- Curd: 2 litres
- Ghee: 500 g
- Banana: 12 bananas (overripe)
- Coconut water: 3 litres
- Sugarcane juice: 3 litres
- Water: sufficient to make 200 litres
Mix all and ferment for 30 days (stir daily for 7 days, then every 3 days)

APPLICATION:
- Foliar spray: 3% solution (3 litres Panchagavya in 97 litres water)
- Soil drench: 200 litres/acre
- Seed treatment: Soak seed in 3% solution for 20 minutes before sowing
- Apply every 15–20 days from germination to harvest
- Results: 15–20% yield improvement over non-treated organic crops

2.3 JEEVAMRIT (Zero Budget Natural Farming):
Popularised by Subhash Palekar. ZBNF/Natural Farming — practised widely in AP and Karnataka.

PREPARATION (200 litres):
- Cow dung (local Desi breed only): 10 kg
- Cow urine: 10 litres
- Jaggery: 2 kg
- Pulse flour (besan or any): 2 kg
- Soil from tree base (native microorganism source): 1 handful
- Water: to make 200 litres

FERMENTATION: Stir and ferment in shade for 48 hours in summer, 72 hours in winter

APPLICATION:
- Soil application: 200 litres per acre (once a month)
- As seed treatment: Dilute 1:10 with water, soak seed 6 hours
- As foliar: 10 litres Jeevamrit in 190 litres water (5% solution)

SCIENTIFIC BASIS:
- Cow dung contains 300–500 crore (3–5 billion) microorganisms per gram
- Fermentation multiplies these: 500 crore per ml after 48 hours
- These microorganisms solubilize locked phosphorus, potassium, zinc in soil
- Fix atmospheric nitrogen (Azotobacter, Azospirillum present naturally)

2.4 BEEJAMRIT (Seed Treatment in ZBNF):
- Cow dung: 5 kg
- Cow urine: 5 litres
- Lime: 50 g (dissolved in 2 litres water)
- Soil from tree base: 1 handful
- Water: 20 litres
Mix and allow to stand 24 hours. Dip seed and dry in shade before sowing.
Prevents seed-borne diseases naturally.

SECTION 3: ORGANIC PEST MANAGEMENT
=======================================================================

3.1 NEEM-BASED PESTICIDES:
Most versatile organic pesticide — affects 600+ pest species.

NEEM SEED KERNEL EXTRACT (NSKE):
- Crush 5 kg neem seeds + soak in 10 litres water + 50 g soap (overnight)
- Filter and spray at 5% (5 litres NSKE in 95 litres water)
- Effective against: sucking pests, bollworm, leaf miner, aphids, thrips
- Repellent and anti-feedant — pests don't die immediately but stop feeding

NEEM OIL (Commercial — 3,000 ppm azadirachtin):
- Mix 3 ml neem oil + 1 ml liquid soap + 1 litre water
- Spray on leaf underside especially (contact insecticide)

NEEMASTRA:
- Cow urine: 10 litres + Neem leaves (crushed): 5 kg
- Mix and ferment 48 hours
- Dilute 10:90 (1 litre in 9 litres water) and spray
- Effective against sucking pests and soft-bodied insects

3.2 DASHAPARNI ARK (10-leaf extract):
Uses 10 specific plants (each toxic to pests):
Neem, Dhatura, Papaya, Pomegranate, Custard apple, Guava, Lantana,
Karanj, Marigold, and one local toxic plant.

PREPARATION:
- Collect 2 kg leaves of each of the above 10 plants
- Crush and soak in 200 litres water + 2 kg cow dung + 2 litres cow urine
- Ferment 60 days (stirring daily for first 15 days)
- Filter and use at 3% dilution

APPLICATION: Effective against most Lepidopteron and sucking pests.

3.3 BOTANICAL PESTICIDES (APPROVED FOR ORGANIC USE):
- Pyrethrin (Chrysanthemum flowers): Broad-spectrum | Fast knockdown
- Azadirachtin (Neem-based): OMRI listed, globally approved
- Spinosad (from Saccharopolyspora spinosa): Approved for organic
- Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt): For caterpillar control
- Beauveria bassiana: Fungal bioagent for insects
- Metarhizium anisopliae: For soil insects (white grubs, termites)

3.4 PHEROMONE TRAPS:
- Species-specific: Different lures for different pests
- No pesticide residue risk
- Monitoring + mass trapping
- Most cost-effective when used systematically

3.5 TRAP CROPS:
- Marigold border: Repels many sucking pests; attracts Helicoverpa egg
  * 3 rows of marigold for every 30 rows of crop
- Castor border: Helicoverpa prefers castor → destroys eggs on castor
- Mustard in tomato/cabbage: Blue butterfly trap crop

SECTION 4: ORGANIC CERTIFICATION IN INDIA
=======================================================================

4.1 PGS-INDIA (Participatory Guarantee System):
- For small and marginal farmers
- FREE certification — group-based
- Recognised by Government of India (Ministry of Agriculture)
- Requirements: 3-year conversion period, maintain records
- Process: Register at local agriculture office or online: www.pgsindia-ncof.gov.in

4.2 NPOP (National Programme for Organic Production):
- For export-oriented farmers
- More stringent — international recognition
- Accepted by EU, USA, Japan, Switzerland
- Third-party certification: APEDA-accredited certifiers

4.3 CONVERSION PERIOD:
- 3 years of organic management before certification
- During this period: No chemical input allowed
- Yield may drop 15–30% during conversion
- Support: Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana (PKVY) — Rs 31,500/ha/3 years
  subsidy for organic farmers

4.4 IMPORTANT SCHEMES:
- PKVY (Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana): Rs 31,500/ha support over 3 years
- NMoOP (National Mission on Oilseeds and Oil Palm): Organic component
- PM-PRANAM: Fertilizer subsidy reduction linked to organic promotion
- State-specific: Sikkim (100% organic state), Himachal Pradesh organic policy

SECTION 5: INTEGRATED NUTRIENT MANAGEMENT (INM)
=======================================================================

INM combines organic and inorganic inputs optimally.
Not purely organic, but highly beneficial and sustainable.

STEP 1: Soil test (mandatory)
STEP 2: Apply FYM/Compost to meet 50% of nitrogen requirement
STEP 3: Apply 50% of recommended chemical N + P + K
STEP 4: Use biofertilizers (Rhizobium, Azospirillum, PSB) to fix remaining N

BENEFITS:
- 25–30% reduction in chemical fertilizer cost
- Maintains soil organic carbon
- Sustainable over long term
- No yield penalty (unlike full organic)
