ICAR TECHNICAL BULLETIN — Groundnut (Peanut) Cultivation for Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, and Tamil Nadu
Source: ICAR-Directorate of Groundnut Research (DGR), Junagadh, Gujarat
Document Reference: ICAR-DGR/Tech/2024/GJ-AP-TN-01

India is the world's second-largest groundnut producer. Gujarat alone contributes 40% of India's
groundnut production. Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, and Rajasthan are other major producers.
Groundnut provides edible oil (50% of India's edible oil demand from groundnut), protein-rich cake,
and fodder — a complete crop system.

=======================================================================
SECTION 1: RECOMMENDED VARIETIES
=======================================================================

GUJARAT:
1. GG 20 (Gujarat Groundnut 20):
   - Duration: 100–105 days | Yield: 25–30 q/ha
   - Bold seeds, high oil content (50%)
   - Blight resistant

2. GG 2 (Gujarat Groundnut 2):
   - Duration: 105–110 days | Yield: 22–27 q/ha
   - Most popular in Saurashtra

3. GPBD 4 (Girnar 4):
   - Late leaf spot and rust resistant
   - Yield: 25–35 q/ha | Kharif season

4. Somnath (GG 11): Rain-fed suitable

ANDHRA PRADESH:
1. K 6: Most popular | Yield: 25–30 q/ha
2. TCGS 29: High yielding | Drought tolerant
3. K 9 (Greeshma): Summer crop, short duration
4. TMV 2: Bunchy top resistant

TAMIL Nadu:
1. VRI 2: Duration 90 days | Summer crop
2. Co 2 (Chandra): High yielding
3. TMV 7: Blight resistant, popular

RAJASTHAN / MP:
1. R 9251: Drought tolerant | Short duration
2. Chandra (TG 37A): Widely adapted

=======================================================================
SECTION 2: PLANTING SEASON AND PREPARATION
=======================================================================

SEASONS:
| State      | Kharif               | Rabi/Summer        |
|-----------|---------------------|--------------------|
| Gujarat    | June 15 – July 10   | Jan 15 – Feb 28    |
| AP         | June 15 – July 10   | Nov 1 – Dec 15     |
| Tamil Nadu | June–July           | Dec–Jan (winter)   |

FIELD PREPARATION:
- Deep ploughing 20–25 cm (for pod penetration into soil)
- Sandy loam soil preferred (light soil easier for pod development)
- Apply gypsum 400 kg/ha as basal (calcium is critical for peg development)

SEED RATE:
- Bold seeded varieties: 100–120 kg/ha (shelled seed)
- Small seeded: 80–90 kg/ha

SEED TREATMENT:
1. Thiram 75% WS: 3 g/kg seed — fungicide
2. Rhizobium inoculum (Bradyrhizobium japonicum): 200 g/10 kg seed
   - Mix with 10% jaggery solution as sticker
   - Apply just before sowing (morning of sowing day)
3. Trichoderma viride: 4 g/kg seed (collar rot prevention)

SPACING:
- Kharif: 30 cm × 15 cm (22,222 plants/ha) or 45 cm × 10 cm
- Summer: 30 cm × 10 cm (higher density for summer crop)

=======================================================================
SECTION 3: FERTILIZER MANAGEMENT
=======================================================================

RECOMMENDED DOSE:
| Nutrient | Amount     | Notes                                  |
|---------|-----------|----------------------------------------|
| N       | 20 kg/ha  | Starter dose only; Rhizobium fixes rest|
| P₂O₅   | 40 kg/ha  | Basal (SSP preferred over DAP)         |
| K₂O    | 40 kg/ha  | Basal                                  |
| Ca      | 400 kg/ha | As gypsum — applied at flowering       |
| S       | 25 kg/ha  | As gypsum (S-containing fertilizer)    |
| B       | 1 kg/ha   | As Borax — at pegging                 |

GYPSUM APPLICATION — CRITICAL FOR GROUNDNUT:
- Apply 400–500 kg/ha at flowering / pegging stage (50–55 DAS)
- Gypsum provides calcium directly to developing pods
- Without calcium → "pops" (empty pods) / "hollow heart" disorder
- Apply in top 10 cm soil around plants (broadcast between rows)

MICRONUTRIENT MANAGEMENT:
- Iron deficiency (common in calcareous soils): 
  FeSO₄ 0.5% spray (2 rounds at 30 and 45 DAS)
- Zinc deficiency: ZnSO₄ 0.5% spray or 25 kg/ha soil application
- Boron deficiency: Borax 0.2% spray at pegging (improves pod fill)

=======================================================================
SECTION 4: IRRIGATION
=======================================================================

CRITICAL STAGES:
1. Pegging stage: 30–35 DAS — critical
2. Pod formation: 45–60 DAS — most critical
3. Pod filling: 60–90 DAS — critical

Rain-fed crop: 450–550 mm rainfall sufficient
Irrigated: 5–6 irrigations at 10–14 day intervals during dry periods

AVOID:
- Irrigation within 10–15 days of harvest (causes aflatoxin contamination)
- Waterlogged conditions (promotes collar rot)

=======================================================================
SECTION 5: MAJOR DISEASES
=======================================================================

5.1 TIKKA DISEASE — EARLY LEAF SPOT (Cercospora arachidicola)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Brown-black circular spots on upper leaf surface
- Yellow halo around spots
- Defoliation in severe cases — significant yield loss

5.2 LATE LEAF SPOT (Cercosporidium personatum)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Darker, rougher texture spots than early leaf spot
- No yellow halo
- More severe than early leaf spot

MANAGEMENT (both leaf spots):
- Spray schedule starting at 35 DAS:
  * Mancozeb 75 WP: 2.5 g/litre (preventive, 3 sprays at 15-day intervals)
  * Chlorothalonil 75 WP: 2 g/litre
  * Tebuconazole 25.9 EC: 1 ml/litre (curative)
- Grow resistant varieties: GPBD 4, JL 24 (late leaf spot resistant)

5.3 GROUNDNUT RUST (Puccinia arachidis)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Orange-yellow pustules on lower leaf surface
- Yellow spots on upper surface
- Occurs from August onwards (late Kharif)

MANAGEMENT:
- Mancozeb 75 WP + Chlorothalonil tank mix spray
- Triadimefon 25 WP: 1 g/litre (curative)
- GPBD 4 variety is rust-resistant

5.4 COLLAR ROT / STEM ROT (Sclerotium rolfsii)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Browning and rotting of stem at soil level
- White mycelial growth (cotton-like) on stem base
- Mustard-like brown sclerotia on and near the stem
- Sudden wilting of plant (not seen in early morning)

MANAGEMENT:
- Avoid waterlogging conditions
- Seed treatment with Trichoderma viride 4 g/kg seed (CRITICAL)
- Soil application: Trichoderma viride 2.5 kg/ha mixed with 250 kg FYM
- Carbendazim 50 WP: 2 g/litre drench around affected plants

5.5 AFLATOXIN CONTAMINATION (Aspergillus flavus)
Critical food safety issue — not visible in the field.

PREVENTION:
- Harvest on time (avoid over-maturity in field)
- Harvest when soil moisture is moderate
- Do NOT store above 9% moisture
- Store in cool, dry, well-ventilated conditions
- Aflatoxin testing: Any batch above 30 ppb REJECTED by processors

=======================================================================
SECTION 6: MAJOR PESTS
=======================================================================

6.1 LEAF MINER (Aproaerema modicella)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Larvae mine inside leaf tissue — visible winding mines
- Leaf dries and curls (cigar-like rolling at severe stages)

MANAGEMENT:
- Chlorpyrifos 20 EC: 1 litre/acre
- Quinalphos 25 EC: 1 litre/acre

6.2 SPODOPTERA LITURA (Tobacco Caterpillar)
- Same management as for soybean (see soybean advisory)
- ETL: 4 larvae/metre row length

6.3 THRIPS (Frankliniella schultzei)
- Spread groundnut bud necrosis virus
- Management: Imidacloprid seed treatment (most effective)

6.4 WHITE GRUBS — ROOT FEEDING
- Management: Chlorpyrifos 20 EC 3 litres/ha + sand drench at sowing

=======================================================================
SECTION 7: HARVEST AND POST-HARVEST
=======================================================================

HARVEST TIMING:
- Maturity indicators: 70% pods have dark (tannin-stained) interiors
- Leaf yellowing and shedding
- Shelling check: Seeds fill pods completely, seed coat papery

DIGGING:
- Groundnut digger or tractor-mounted digger
- Allow to dry in field (windrow) for 3–4 days

DRYING AND STORAGE:
- Dry pods to <9% moisture (critical for aflatoxin prevention)
- Store in perforated gunny bags in elevated, ventilated sheds
- Do NOT store in polythene bags (traps moisture — promotes aflatoxin)

MSP 2024-25: ₹6,783/quintal
Oil yield (expeller pressing): 35–42% of weight
