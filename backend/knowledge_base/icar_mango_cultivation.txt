ICAR TECHNICAL BULLETIN — Mango Cultivation Advisory for Uttar Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, and Gujarat
Source: ICAR-Central Institute for Subtropical Horticulture (CISH), Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh
Document Reference: ICAR-CISH/Tech/2024/IN-01

India is the world's largest mango producer — 20 million tonnes from 25 lakh hectares.
Uttar Pradesh (Malihabad Belt), Andhra Pradesh (Krishnapatnam), Maharashtra (Konkan — Alphonso),
Gujarat (Gir Kesar), Tamil Nadu, and Bihar are major producing states.
Mango is the national fruit of India.

SECTION 1: MAJOR VARIETIES BY STATE
=======================================================================

UTTAR PRADESH (Lucknow / Malihabad Belt):
1. Dasheri: Famous for aroma, sweetest taste
   - Originated in Malihabad | Season: July
   - GI Tagged — Malihabad Dasheri
   
2. Langra: Kashi-origin | Fibreless, juicy
   - Season: July–August
   
3. Chausa: Season: August–September (late variety)

5. Lucknow 49 (Malda): Excellent shelf life

ANDHRA PRADESH / TELANGANA:
1. Banganapalli (Benishan): Most popular variety in India
   - GI Tagged | Large fruit (300–500 g)
   - Season: May–June | Sweet, fibreless
   
2. Totapuri: Large, parrot-beak shaped
   - Main variety for processing/pickle industry
   - Season: May–June
   
3. Neelum: Late variety (July) | Sweet, fibreless
4. Suvarnarekha: Medium-large | Red blush
5. Raspuri: Karnataka / AP border — excellent quality

MAHARASHTRA (Konkan Belt — Ratnagiri / Sindhudurg):
1. Alphonso (Hapus): World's most premium mango
   - GI Tagged — Ratnagiri/Devgad Alphonso
   - Weight: 200–300 g | Rich, creamy, non-fibrous
   - Season: April–May | Price: Rs 500–2000/kg
   
2. Kesar (Gujarat Origin): Second most premium
   - Grown in Valsad (Maharashtra) and Girnar (Gujarat)
   
3. Payri (Maharashtra): Early season, processing

BIHAR / JHARKHAND:

SECTION 2: ORCHARD ESTABLISHMENT
=======================================================================

CLIMATE REQUIREMENTS:
- Ideal temperature: 24–27°C (annual mean)
- Low/no rain during flowering (Nov–Feb): Rains cause anthracnose
- High temperature and dry weather during fruit development
- Mango cannot tolerate frost (kills young plants)

SOIL REQUIREMENTS:
- Well-drained deep loam or sandy loam
- pH: 5.5–7.5 (ideal 6.0–7.0)
- AVOID: Heavy clay, waterlogged soils
- Can tolerate light salinity (up to 2 dS/m)

PLANTING:
- Season: June–July (SW monsoon) or February–March (spring)
- Spacing: 10 m × 10 m (100 plants/ha) — traditional
  High Density: 2.5 m × 5 m (800 plants/ha) — Ultrahigh density orchards
- Pit size: 1 m × 1 m × 1 m
- Fill pit: Topsoil + 25 kg FYM + 1 kg SSP + 100 g Phorate 10G
- Grafted plants begin bearing in 3–4 years (vs 8–10 years for seedlings)

VARIETIES FOR HIGH DENSITY:
- Amrapali (Dwarf hybrid): Best for HDP
  Yield: 15–20 kg/plant even at HDP (400–600 plants/ha)
- Mallika: High yielding | Semi-dwarf

SECTION 3: FERTILIZER MANAGEMENT
=======================================================================

FERTILIZER DOSE (per tree per year, for bearing trees):

| Tree Age | FYM  | N     | P     | K     |
|---------|------|-------|-------|-------|
| 1 year  | 10 kg| 100 g | 50 g  | 100 g |
| 3 years | 25 kg| 300 g | 150 g | 300 g |
| 5 years | 40 kg| 500 g | 250 g | 500 g |
| 10+ yrs | 50 kg| 1 kg  | 500 g | 1 kg  |

SPLIT APPLICATION:
- June (pre-monsoon onset): 50% N + 100% P + 50% K + FYM
- September (post-monsoon): 50% N + 50% K

CRITICAL MICRONUTRIENTS:
- Zinc: ZnSO₄ 0.5% spray at bud break (March) — prevents little leaf
- Boron: Borax 0.3% spray at flowering — improves fruit set
- Calcium: CaCl₂ 0.5% spray at marble stage — prevents spongy tissue (Alphonso)

SPONGY TISSUE (Alphonso-specific disorder):
- Internal brownish spongy areas despite good external appearance
- Cause: calcium deficiency + high temperature during development
- Prevention: CaCl₂ 0.5% spray at 3 intervals during development

=======================================================================
SECTION 4: FLOWERING MANAGEMENT
=======================================================================

MANGO ALTERNATE BEARING PROBLEM:
Many mango varieties (especially Alphonso, Dasheri) bear heavily one year
and poorly the next — "Alternate Bearing" or "Off Year" problem.

SOLUTIONS:
1. Cultar (Paclobutrazol) treatment:
   - Method: Soil drench or trunk smear
   - Dose: 4.5 g a.i./tree (soil application, September–October of off year)
   - Effect: Induces early, uniform flowering in off year
   - Used commercially in Konkan for Alphonso

2. Potassium nitrate (KNO₃) spray:
   - 2% spray at tip of branches (just before expected flowering)
   - Induces uniform bud break

3. Smudging: Burning agricultural waste near trees (smoke triggers flowering)

PROBLEMS AT FLOWERING:
- Spongy Blossom Midge: Removes flowers
- Mango Hopper: Attacks panicles and flowers

SECTION 5: MAJOR DISEASES
=======================================================================

5.1 ANTHRACNOSE (Colletotrichum gloeosporioides)
MOST COMMON DISEASE — attacks flowers, young fruits, leaves.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Dark brown to black irregular spots on leaves, twigs, flowers
- "Blossom Blight" — flowers turn brown and drop
- Black sunken spots on fruits (pre and post-harvest)
- Severely affects fruit quality and marketability

MANAGEMENT:
- Avoid overhead irrigation during flowering
- Copper oxychloride 50 WP: 3 g/litre spray at panicle emergence
- Carbendazim 50 WP: 1 g/litre spray (2 rounds — pre and post-rain)
- Propiconazole 25 EC: 1 ml/litre for curative action
- Post-harvest: Hot water treatment 52°C for 5 minutes + Carbendazim dip

5.2 POWDERY MILDEW (Oidium mangiferae)
IDENTIFICATION:
- White powdery coating on young leaves, panicles, and young fruits
- Severe in cool, humid nights (December–February)
- Panicle blight — entire flower cluster affected

MANAGEMENT:
- Sulphur 80 WP: 2 g/litre spray (most effective, safe at pre-bloom)
- Triadimefon 25 WP: 1 g/litre
- Hexaconazole 5 SC: 1 ml/litre
- First spray at panicle emergence + 2nd at 15-day interval

5.3 SOOTY MOULD (Capnodium mangiferae)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Black soot-like growth on leaves and fruits
- Grows on honeydew excreted by hoppers/mealybugs

MANAGEMENT:
- Control insect vector (hoppers, mealybugs)
- Starch solution 4%: Spray to dislodge sooty mould from leaves

5.4 STEM END ROT (Botryodiplodia theobromae)
POST-HARVEST disease — common in transport

MANAGEMENT:
- Thiabendazole or Carbendazim post-harvest dip
- Harvest with full stalk attached (2 cm)

SECTION 6: MAJOR PESTS
=======================================================================

6.1 MANGO HOPPER (Idioscopus clypealis, Amritodus atkinsoni)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Small pale green/yellow wedge-shaped insects
- Adults and nymphs suck sap from panicles and young leaves
- Cause "hopperburn" — blackening of affected tissue
- Exude honeydew → sooty mould

MANAGEMENT:
- Imidacloprid 17.8 SL: 0.5 ml/litre spray at panicle emergence
- Thiamethoxam 25 WG: 0.25 g/litre
- First spray at panicle emergence (before full opening)

6.2 MANGO STONE WEEVIL (Sternochetus mangiferae)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Grubs feed on seed (stone) inside the fruit
- External fruit appears normal
- Quarantine pest — restricts export

MANAGEMENT:
- Hot water treatment 52°C/10 minutes (post-harvest)
- Avoid mixing infested fruits with clean ones
- Collect and destroy fallen fruits (breeding site)

6.3 FRUIT FLY (Bactrocera dorsalis)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Flies lay eggs in ripening fruits
- Maggots cause internal rotting
- Pin-hole visible on fruit surface (oviposition site)

MANAGEMENT:
- Methyl Eugenol traps (Cue-lure): 25 traps/ha (replace lure every 21 days)
- Protein bait spray: Spinosad 0.02% + Hydrolysed protein 1% bait
- Bag fruits individually at pea size (small orchards)
- Collect and destroy fallen fruits daily

6.4 MANGO MEALYBUG (Drosicha mangiferae)
IDENTIFICATION:
- White cottony mass at base of buds, stems, and on panicles
- Severely affects young trees and panicles

MANAGEMENT:
- Sticky trap bands (alkathene sheet 20 cm wide) around trunk base (October)
- Spray Imidacloprid 17.8 SL + petroleum oil spray
- Remove weeds around tree (nymph habitat)

=======================================================================
SECTION 7: HARVESTING AND POST-HARVEST
=======================================================================

MATURITY INDICES:
- Alphonso: Changes from green to yellow; stalk turns yellow; floats in water
- Dasheri: Slight yellow tinge; plum aroma
- Banganapalli: Shoulder development; slight colour change
- Specific gravity: < 1.0 (mature fruit floats in water)

POST-HARVEST HANDLING:
- Harvest in cool hours (morning)
- Leave 2–5 cm stalk (prevents latex drip on fruit surface)
- Wash latex with 1% lime solution or plain water
- Grade by weight: A (>300g), B (200–300g), C (<200g)
- Pre-cooling: Forced air cooling at 10–12°C extends shelf life

EXPORT PROTOCOL:
- Vapour heat treatment (VHT): 46°C for 10 minutes — for Japan, S. Korea export
- Hot water treatment: 52°C / 5 minutes — for USA, EU export
- Residue monitoring: Test for Imidacloprid, Cypermethrin (most common violations)

PRICES:
- Alphonso (Ratnagiri): Rs 800–2500/dozen at farm gate
- Kesar (Junagadh): Rs 60–120/kg
- Dasheri (Malihabad): Rs 30–60/kg
- Banganapalli: Rs 30–50/kg
