ICAR TECHNICAL BULLETIN — Himalayan High-Altitude Horticulture: Apple, Walnut & Saffron
Source: ICAR-Central Institute of Temperate Horticulture (CITH), Old Air Field, Rangreth, Srinagar (J&K)
Document Reference: ICAR-CITH/Tech/2024/JK-HP-UK-01

The Northwestern Himalayan region (Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, and Uttarakhand) produces over
98% of India's temperate fruits, nuts, and heritage saffron. Temperate horticulture is the economic lifeline
of hill communities. This bulletin provides verified protocols for high-density apple orchards, walnut grafting,
and saffron rejuvenation under the National Saffron Mission.

=======================================================================
SECTION 1: HIGH-DENSITY APPLE ORCHARDING (J&K, HP, Uttarakhand)
=======================================================================

1.1 TRADITIONAL VS HIGH-DENSITY (HDP) APPLE ORCHARDS:
- Traditional seedling orchards: 200–250 trees/ha; gestation 8–10 years; yield 8–12 t/ha; difficult spray coverage.
- High-Density Orchards (HDP): 1,600 to 3,333 trees/ha on clonal rootstocks; gestation 2–3 years; yield 35–50 t/ha;
  trellis-supported canopy; uniform sunlight penetration; 95% 'A' grade export fruit color.

1.2 CLONAL ROOTSTOCKS & VARIETIES:
- Clonal Rootstocks:
  * M.9 (Malling 9): True dwarf, highest yield efficiency, requires permanent trellis wire support.
  * MM.106 (Malling Merton 106): Semi-dwarf, better anchorage, suitable for hill slopes and lighter soils.
  * MM.111: Vigorous, highly drought-hardy, suited for rainfed undulating terrains.
- Premium HDP Varieties:
  * Gala Series: Buckeye Gala, Gala Brookfield, Dark Baron Gala (early harvest: August, high market demand).
  * Fuji Series: Red Fuji, Aztec Fuji (late harvest: October, exceptional crispness and 6-month cold storage life).
  * Red Delicious Sports: Super Chief, Scarlet Spur-II, Red Velox (deep red solid blush, sweet taste).
  * Granny Smith: Crisp green apple, outstanding pollinizer variety.
- Pollinizer Proportion: Minimum 15–20% pollinizers (Granny Smith, Golden Delicious, Manchurian Crab Apple)
  must be interspersed; install 4–6 honeybee boxes/ha during 10% bloom for guaranteed fruit set.

1.3 PRUNING, TRAINING & CANOPY ARCHITECTURE:
- Tall Spindle System: Spacing 3 m between rows × 1 m between trees (3,333 trees/ha).
- Retain central leader straight up to 3.5 m height; branch angles tied down to 110–120° (below horizontal).
- Branch tying induces flower buds while preventing vegetative wood vigour.
- Drip Irrigation & Fertigation: Mandatory for HDP; water requirement 25–35 L/tree/day during fruit sizing (June–July).

1.4 APPLE SCAB (Venturia inaequalis) — THE #1 THREAT:
- Symptoms: Olive-green velvety spots on leaves and fruit which turn corky, scabbed, and cracked;
  fruit becomes unmarketable. Spores overwinter in fallen dead leaves on orchard floor.
- Preventive & Eradicative Spray Schedule (SKUAST-K / Dr YS Parmar UHF Solan Approved):
  1. Silver Tip stage: Captan 50 WP (2.5 g/L) or Mancozeb 75 WP (3.0 g/L).
  2. Pink Bud stage: Difenoconazole 25 EC (0.3 ml/L) or Zineb 75 WP (2.5 g/L).
  3. Petal Fall (Critical): Hexaconazole 5 SC (1.0 ml/L) or Trifloxystrobin + Tebuconazole (0.4 g/L).
  4. Fruit Development (Pea size): Kresoxim-methyl 44.3 SC (0.5 ml/L) or Dodine 65 WP (0.75 g/L).
- Orchard Sanitation: Spray 5% Urea on fallen leaves in November to accelerate microbial decomposition of scab perithecia.

1.5 OTHER MAJOR APPLE DISORDERS:
- San Jose Scale (Quadraspidiotus perniciosus): Ashy scale encrustations on bark and fruit with red rings.
  * Management: Apply Dormant Tree Spray Oil (Horticultural Mineral Oil @ 20 ml/L) in February before bud break.
- Woolly Apple Aphid (Eriosoma lanigerum): White cottony tufts on stem wounds and roots causing galls.
  * Management: Release bioagent Aphelinus mali (parasitoid wasp); drench Chlorpyrifos 20 EC (2 ml/L) around root collar.
- Alternaria Leaf Blotch: Spray Mancozeb 75 WP (2.5 g/L) or Azoxystrobin (1 ml/L) post-monsoon.

SECTION 2: WALNUT PROPAGATION & MANAGEMENT (J&K, Himachal Pradesh)
=======================================================================

Jammu & Kashmir accounts for 90% of India's walnut production. Historically, 95% was grown from seed,
resulting in 15-year bearing delay and erratic nut quality.

2.1 GRAFTED COMMERCIAL VARIETIES:
- CITH-Walnut-1, CITH-Walnut-2, Sulaiman, Hamdan, Opex Caulcher.
- Characteristics: Paper shell (easily cracked by hand), kernel recovery >50%, light amber/extra-light kernel color.

2.2 BENCH & POLYHOUSE GRAFTING:
- Traditional open field grafting has <15% success due to fluctuating mountain temperatures.
- ICAR-CITH Technique (Wedge / Tongue grafting inside Polyhouse under controlled temp 26–28°C & 80% RH):
  * Success rate increases to 75–85%.
  * Grafted walnut plants start bearing commercial nuts in the 4th year (vs 12–15 years for seedling trees).

2.3 WALNUT BLIGHT & HULL ROT:
- Walnut Anthracnose / Blight (Gnomonia leptostyla):
  * Dark brown necrotic spots on leaves and green husk; premature nut drop.
  * Management: Spray Copper Oxychloride 50 WP (3 g/L) or Bordeaux Mixture 1% at catkin emergence and post-bloom.

SECTION 3: KASHMIRI SAFFRON (Crocus sativus L.) CULTIVATION
=======================================================================

Kashmir Saffron (Pampore, Pulwama, Budgam, Kishtwar) is globally renowned for highest crocin content (color),
safranal (aroma), and picrocrocin (bitterness). Received GI Tag in 2020.

3.1 SOIL & ECOLOGY:
- Karewa soils (lacustrine deep silt-clay loams); pH 7.5–8.2 with high calcium carbonate.
- Altitude: 1,600 to 2,000 meters above sea level. Needs dry summer and snow in winter.

3.2 CORM SELECTION & PLANTING DENSITY:
- Traditional planting: Small corms (<8 g), widely spaced; yield 1.5–2.0 kg dry saffron/ha.
- National Saffron Mission Rejuvenation Protocol:
  * Corm Grade: Select healthy, disease-free corms weighing >10–12 grams (mandatory for 1st year flowering).
  * Corm Treatment: Dip in Carbendazim 50 WP (1 g/L) + Mancozeb (2 g/L) for 15 minutes to eradicate corm rot.
  * Spacing: Raised beds (1.5 m wide); 20 cm row-to-row × 10 cm corm-to-corm; depth 12–15 cm.
  * Corm density: 5.0 to 5.5 lakh corms/ha. Yield surges to 4.5–5.5 kg dry saffron/ha.

3.3 SAFFRON CORM ROT (Fusarium oxysporum / Rhizoctonia crocorum):
- The most destructive disease, responsible for 60% decline in traditional acreage.
- Symptoms: Yellowing of foliar foliage in spring; rotting of underground corms with dark sunken lesions.
- Integrated Management:
  * Solarize nursery beds under transparent polythene for 40 days during June–July.
  * Soil application of Trichoderma viride enriched FYM @ 5 kg/bed.
  * Drench Carbendazim 0.1% or Copper Oxychloride 0.3% if rot patches appear in field.

3.4 FLOWER PICKING & DRYING PROTOCOL:
- Flowering window: Mid-October to Mid-November (approx 20–25 days).
- Pick flowers early in the morning before sun opens petals completely to preserve volatile safranal aroma.
- Stigma Separation: Separate tripartite crimson stigmas from yellow styles on the same day.
- Drying: Traditional shade drying takes 4–5 days and degrades crocin.
  * Modern Solar-assisted vacuum tunnel dryers (ICAR/Saffron Park Dusoo): Dry at 45–50°C in 3–4 hours.
  * Moisture reduced to 8% while retaining grade-1 crocin color strength (>220 points).
- Market Value: Pure GI-certified Kashmiri Mongra Saffron commands ₹2,50,000 to ₹3,50,000 per kg.
