ICAR TECHNICAL BULLETIN — Mustard (Rapeseed-Mustard) Cultivation for Rajasthan, Haryana, and Madhya Pradesh
Source: ICAR-Directorate of Rapeseed-Mustard Research (DRMR), Bharatpur, Rajasthan
Document Reference: ICAR-DRMR/Tech/2024/RJ-HR-MP-01

Mustard (Brassica juncea) is India's most important oilseed after soybean. Rajasthan alone contributes
46% of India's mustard production. UP, Haryana, MP, and West Bengal are other major producers.
India produces 8–9 million tonnes of mustard annually. Mustard oil is the primary cooking oil in
North India (used by over 400 million people daily).

=======================================================================
SECTION 1: RECOMMENDED VARIETIES
=======================================================================

RAJASTHAN:
1. RH 749 (Giriraj): Most popular in Rajasthan
   - Duration: 125–130 days | Yield: 22–28 q/ha
   - Bold seed | High oil content (40%)
   - White rust resistant
   
2. RH 30: Old reliable variety | Yield 18–22 q/ha
3. NRC DR-2 (Navgold): High oil (42%) | Sclerotinia resistant
4. RH 0749: Early maturing

HARYANA:
1. RH 8113: Most popular | Yield 20–25 q/ha
2. PHR 126 (Pusa Bold): Bold seed, good oil quality
3. Varuna (T 59): Classic variety, widely adapted

UTTAR PRADESH:
1. Pusa Bold (Bio-902): High yielding | Aphid tolerant
2. Kranti (MLK 2): Suitable for late sowing

MADHYA PRADESH:
1. JM 1: Good for black cotton soil
2. Ashirwad (JD 6): Yield 20–22 q/ha

=======================================================================
SECTION 2: SOWING AND AGRONOMY
=======================================================================

SOWING DATES:
| State           | Optimal            | Late               |
|----------------|-------------------|--------------------|
| Rajasthan      | 1–25 October       | After 1 November   |
| Haryana/Delhi  | 1–15 October       | After 25 October   |
| UP             | 5–25 October       | After 1 November   |
| MP             | 10 Oct – 5 Nov     | After 15 November  |

EACH WEEK DELAY = 2–3 q/ha YIELD LOSS.

SEED RATE:
- Timely sowing: 4 kg/ha (broadcast) or 3 kg/ha (line sowing)
- Late sowing: 5–6 kg/ha
- Line sowing with seed drill gives 15–20% higher yield than broadcast

SPACING: 45 cm × 15 cm (line sowing)

SEED TREATMENT:
1. Thiram 75% WS: 3 g/kg seed (seed-borne Alternaria prevention)
2. Imidacloprid 70% WS: 5 ml/kg seed (aphid and diamond back moth protection)

FIELD PREPARATION:
- 1 deep ploughing + 2 light harrowings
- Apply FYM 10–15 t/ha as basal
- Avoid excessively fine seedbed (causes soil crusting after rain)

=======================================================================
SECTION 3: FERTILIZER MANAGEMENT
=======================================================================

RECOMMENDED DOSE:
| Nutrient | Irrigated   | Rain-fed   |
|---------|------------|-----------|
| N       | 80–100 kg  | 40 kg     |
| P₂O₅   | 40–60 kg   | 30 kg     |
| K₂O    | 30–40 kg   | 20 kg     |
| S       | 20–40 kg   | 20 kg     |

SULPHUR IS CRITICAL FOR MUSTARD:
- Mustard is a high sulphur-demanding crop (glucosinolate synthesis)
- Deficiency: Yellowing of young leaves, reduced oil content
- Apply 20–40 kg S/ha as gypsum (single super phosphate also contains S)
- Foliar: Ammonium sulphate 2% spray at flowering

SPLIT APPLICATION OF N:
- Basal: 50% N + full P + full K
- At first irrigation (25–30 DAS): 50% N

BORON APPLICATION:
- 0.2% Borax spray at flowering stage
- Improves siliqua (pod) set significantly

=======================================================================
SECTION 4: IRRIGATION
=======================================================================

- Mustard is moderately drought-tolerant
- 2 irrigations give highest efficiency:
  1. At 30–35 DAS (rosette/branching stage) — most critical
  2. At 55–60 DAS (flowering stage)
- Do NOT irrigate at podding/grain filling (lodging risk)
- Excess irrigation after pod formation → lodging and Sclerotinia

=======================================================================
SECTION 5: MAJOR DISEASES
=======================================================================

5.1 ALTERNARIA BLIGHT (Alternaria brassicae and A. brassicicola)
MOST COMMON disease of mustard.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Dark brown to black circular spots on leaves, stems, and siliquae (pods)
- Yellow halo around spots (on leaves)
- Severe on siliquae — seeds shrivel, oil content drops
- Worse after morning dew or intermittent rains

MANAGEMENT:
- Seed treatment with Thiram (preventive)
- First spray at 50% flowering: Iprodione 50 WP 1 g/litre
- Second spray at siliqua formation: Mancozeb 75 WP 2.5 g/litre
- Chlorothalonil 75 WP: 2 g/litre as alternative
- Avoid overhead irrigation (sprinkler wets leaves → promotes Alternaria)

5.2 WHITE RUST (Albugo candida)
IDENTIFICATION:
- White blister-like pustules on lower leaf surface
- Yellowing on upper surface above pustules
- "Staghead" deformation of inflorescence (most characteristic)
- Severe in cool, humid conditions (November–December)

MANAGEMENT:
- Metalaxyl-M + Mancozeb: 2 g/litre spray at first appearance
- Grow resistant varieties: RH 749 (moderately resistant)
- Avoid excess nitrogen and waterlogging

5.3 SCLEROTINIA STEM ROT (Sclerotinia sclerotiorum)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Water-soaked lesion on stem — rapidly girdling the stem
- White cotton-like mycelium + black rat-dropping sclerotia inside stem
- Plant wilts and falls (lodging of stems)
- Worse in heavy dew, foggy conditions (January–February)

MANAGEMENT:
- Avoid dense sowing and waterlogging
- Carbendazim 50 WP: 1 g/litre spray (preventive at flowering)
- Tebuconazole 25.9 EC: 1 ml/litre (curative)
- Crop rotation (peas → mustard rotation is HIGH RISK for Sclerotinia)

5.4 DOWNY MILDEW (Peronospora brassicae)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Angular yellow patches on upper leaf surface
- Grey-purple sporulation on lower leaf surface

MANAGEMENT:
- Metalaxyl 0.25% spray at first signs

=======================================================================
SECTION 6: MAJOR PESTS
=======================================================================

6.1 APHIDS (Lipaphis erysimi) — Mustard Aphid
THE MOST IMPORTANT PEST of mustard — can cause 50–70% yield loss.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Greyish-green aphids in large colonies on growing tips and pods
- Yellowing, curling, and stunting of tips
- Honeydew secretion → sooty mould
- Peak damage: January–February (flowering stage)

ETL: 51 aphids per 10 cm tip / plant

MANAGEMENT:
- Pusa Bold variety has some aphid tolerance
- Imidacloprid 17.8 SL: 150 ml/acre spray
- Thiamethoxam 25 WG: 40 g/acre
- Dimethoate 30 EC: 300 ml/acre
- Spray after 3 PM (bees most active before 3 PM — protect pollinators)
- One well-timed spray usually sufficient

NATURAL ENEMIES:
- Syrphid flies, ladybird beetles, parasitoid wasps (Diaeretiella rapae)
- Conserve natural enemies — avoid calendar-based spraying

6.2 DIAMOND BACK MOTH (Plutella xylostella)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Small green caterpillars on leaf underside
- "Window pane" feeding — skeletonize leaves

MANAGEMENT:
- Spinosad 45 SC: 75 ml/acre
- Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt): 1 kg/acre
- Avoid Cypermethrin (resistance developed)

6.3 SAWFLY (Athalia lugens)
- Black and yellow larvae eat leaves
MANAGEMENT: Chlorpyrifos 20 EC 750 ml/acre

=======================================================================
SECTION 7: HARVEST AND PROCESSING
=======================================================================

HARVEST TIMING:
- When 75% of siliquae (pods) turn yellowish-brown
- Seeds inside should be hard and dark brown
- Moisture: 20–22% at harvest
- If delayed: Shattering loss (siliquae burst in hot/windy weather)

THRESHING:
- Combine harvester (set carefully — easy siliqua shattering)
- Cylinder speed: 700–800 RPM
- Traditional: Cut and windrow, then thresh with bullocks/machine

STORAGE:
- Dry to 8–9% moisture
- Use polythene-lined bags or metal bins
- Fumigation: Aluminium phosphide 3 tablets/tonne

OIL EXTRACTION:
- Expeller press: 38–42% oil yield
- Residual cake: 38% protein — valuable animal feed (mustard cake)
