SECTION 1: BLAST DISEASE (Pyricularia oryzae)
=======================================================================

Blast is the most devastating disease of rice worldwide, including Kerala. It is caused by the fungus Pyricularia oryzae.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Leaf blast: Diamond-shaped lesions with grey centre and brown border on leaves
- Neck blast: Rotting at the base of the panicle, causing empty grains
- Node blast: Blackening of internodes leading to collapse
- The disease appears when temperatures are between 22–28°C with high humidity (>90%)

FAVORABLE CONDITIONS:
- Prolonged leaf wetness (>10 hours per day)
- Heavy dew formation
- High nitrogen fertilizer use
- Close planting

MANAGEMENT:
1. Chemical Control:
   - Tricyclazole 75 WP: Apply 0.6 g/litre of water as foliar spray
   - Isoprothiolane 40 EC: 1.5 ml/litre, spray at tillering and panicle initiation
   - Propiconazole 25 EC: 1 ml/litre for neck blast prevention
   
2. Cultural Control:
   - Use blast-resistant varieties: Jyothi, Kanchana, Vyttila-6, Uma
   - Avoid excess nitrogen application (do not exceed 90 kg N/ha)
   - Maintain proper spacing (20 cm × 15 cm)
   - Remove infected plant debris from fields

3. Biological Control:
   - Apply Pseudomonas fluorescens (Pf1) @ 5 g/litre as seed treatment and foliar spray
   - Trichoderma viride seed treatment: 4 g/kg seed

ECONOMIC THRESHOLD:
Spray when 1–2% of leaf area is infected or when neck infection first appears.

SECTION 2: BROWN SPOT (Cochliobolus miyabeanus)
=======================================================================

Brown spot is a fungal disease that causes yield losses of 20–50% in severely affected fields.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Small, circular to oval brown spots on leaves, 1–5 mm diameter
- Spots have yellowish-brown centre with dark brown margin
- Can also infect glumes, causing discoloration of grains
- Symptoms appear on leaves, sheaths, panicles, and grains

PREDISPOSING FACTORS:
- Soil deficiency in potassium and silica
- Waterlogged conditions
- High humidity and warm temperatures (25–30°C)
- Poor soil nutrition is the primary cause

MANAGEMENT:
1. Nutritional Management (most important):
   - Apply potassium fertilizer @ 62 kg K2O/ha
   - Spray 1% KCl (potassium chloride) solution on leaves
   - Apply silica through rice husk ash @ 250 kg/ha
   
2. Chemical Control:
   - Mancozeb 75 WP: 2.5 g/litre foliar spray
   - Iprodione 50 WP: 1 g/litre
   - Edifenphos 50 EC: 1 ml/litre
   
3. Cultural Control:
   - Use balanced fertilizers (avoid excess nitrogen without potassium)
   - Seed treatment with Thiram 75 WS @ 3 g/kg
   - Grow resistant varieties where available

SECTION 3: BACTERIAL LEAF BLIGHT (Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzae)
=======================================================================

Bacterial Leaf Blight (BLB) is a major problem in Kerala during Kharif (Virippu) season.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Water-soaked lesions at leaf margins progressing from tip
- Lesions turn yellowish to white/straw-coloured as they age
- Margins have wavy/irregular border
- 'Kresek' phase: seedlings wilt and die in severe infections
- Bacterial ooze visible as yellowish droplets in early morning

FAVORABLE CONDITIONS:
- High temperature (28–35°C)
- Heavy rainfall and flooding
- High nitrogen application
- Wounded plants after storm damage

MANAGEMENT:
1. Chemical Control:
   - Copper-based bactericides: Copper oxychloride 50 WP @ 3 g/litre
   - Streptomycin sulphate + Tetracycline combination sprays
   - Bleaching powder: Apply to irrigation water at 10 kg/ha
   
2. Cultural Control:
   - Drain water during heavy rain
   - Avoid excess nitrogen (split application of N recommended)
   - Use BLB-resistant varieties: Improved White Ponni, Jyothi, Kanchana
   - Avoid mechanical wounding during cultivation

3. Field Hygiene:
   - Remove and destroy infected leaves and stubble
   - Do not use infected fields for seedbed preparation
   - Clean irrigation channels to prevent spread

SECTION 4: SHEATH BLIGHT (Rhizoctonia solani)
=======================================================================

Sheath blight has become increasingly important in Kerala due to high-yielding variety cultivation.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Oval or irregular greenish-grey lesions on leaf sheath near water line
- Lesions enlarge to form large irregular patches with grey-white centre and dark brown border
- Sclerotia (dark brown mustard-sized structures) form on infected tissue
- Disease progresses upward from sheath to leaves

MANAGEMENT:
1. Chemical Control:
   - Hexaconazole 5 EC: 2 ml/litre, spray at tillering
   - Validamycin 3 SL: 2 ml/litre
   - Propiconazole 25 EC: 1 ml/litre
   - Apply at base of plants near water line for best results
   
2. Cultural Control:
   - Reduce plant density (wider spacing)
   - Drain fields periodically
   - Avoid excess nitrogen
   - Grow moderately resistant varieties

=======================================================================
SECTION 5: FALSE SMUT (Ustilaginoidea virens)
=======================================================================

False smut affects rice panicles and is a warning sign of mycotoxin contamination.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Individual grain kernels replaced by orange-yellow to greenish-black balls (chlamydospores)
- Infected spikelets show green to black velvety balls, 1–4 cm diameter
- Typically 1–5 grains per panicle are infected

MANAGEMENT:
- Propiconazole 25 EC: 1 ml/litre at booting stage
- Copper oxychloride 50 WP: 3 g/litre
- Do not use infected seed
- Diseased grains should not be used for food or feed

=======================================================================
SECTION 6: INTEGRATED DISEASE MANAGEMENT CALENDAR (Kerala)
=======================================================================

VIRIPPU SEASON (June–September):
- Pre-sowing: Seed treatment with Pseudomonas fluorescens or Carbendazim
- Seedling stage (15–20 DAT): Scout for blast; if present, spray Tricyclazole
- Tillering (30–35 DAT): Scout for sheath blight; spray Validamycin if needed
- Panicle initiation (50–55 DAT): Preventive spray for neck blast

MUNDAKAN SEASON (September–December):
- Same scouting protocol
- Extra vigilance for BLB after heavy rains

PUNCHA SEASON (December–February):
- Lower disease pressure due to cooler temperatures
- Main concern: Brown spot in nutritionally deficient soils
