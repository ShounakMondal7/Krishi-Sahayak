ICAR TECHNICAL BULLETIN — Cotton Cultivation Advisory for Vidarbha, Telangana and Gujarat
Source: ICAR-Central Institute for Cotton Research (CICR), Nagpur, Maharashtra
Document Reference: ICAR-CICR/Tech/2024/MH-TS-GJ-01

Cotton is India's primary commercial crop, earning the title "White Gold". India is the world's largest
cotton producer. Maharashtra (Vidarbha), Telangana, Gujarat, and Punjab account for 85% of production.
Bt cotton now covers 95% of India's cotton area.

=======================================================================
SECTION 1: RECOMMENDED COTTON VARIETIES / HYBRIDS
=======================================================================

MAHARASHTRA (Vidarbha):
1. JKCH 1947 (Bt): Most popular in Vidarbha
   - Duration: 170–180 days | Yield: 18–22 q/ha
   - Good bollworm tolerance
   
2. Ankur 651 (Bt): Drought tolerant
   - Yield: 16–20 q/ha | Suitable for rain-fed
   
3. RCH 650 Bt:
   - Yield: 20–25 q/ha | Better fibre quality

TELANGANA / ANDHRA PRADESH:
1. Bunny Bt (Mahyco 915): Most popular
   - Duration: 155–165 days | Yield: 20–28 q/ha
   - Irrigated conditions
   
2. MRC 7017 (Bt): Good for black soils

GUJARAT:
1. Kanchan (Bt): Best for Saurashtra
   - Long staple, good for export quality
   
2. Jadav: Rain-fed, short-duration (150 days)

HARYANA / PUNJAB (Hirsutum):
1. HS 6 (Desi cotton — non-Bt): Rain-fed
2. F 1861 Bt: High-yielding irrigated

SECTION 2: PLANTING SEASON AND ESTABLISHMENT
=======================================================================

SOWING DATES:
| State        | Rain-fed         | Irrigated        |
|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Maharashtra  | June 15 – July 10| May 15 – June 15|
| Telangana    | June 15 – July 1 | May – June       |
| Gujarat      | June 15 – July 15| May – June       |
| Punjab/Haryana| April 25 – May 15| April 15–May 1   |

SPACING:
- Rain-fed black cotton soils: 90 cm × 60 cm (18,500 plants/ha)
- Irrigated hybrids: 90 cm × 90 cm (12,300 plants/ha)
- High Density Planting (HDP): 67 cm × 30 cm (49,500 plants/ha) — new approach

SEED RATE: 1.0–1.5 kg/ha (Bt hybrid, pelleted seed)

NURSERY (for transplanting method):
- Raise in poly bags — reduces transplanting shock
- 30-day old seedlings for transplanting

=======================================================================
SECTION 3: FERTILIZER MANAGEMENT
=======================================================================

RECOMMENDED DOSE PER HECTARE:

| Nutrient | Rain-fed   | Irrigated   |
|---------|-----------|------------|
| N       | 60 kg      | 120–150 kg  |
| P₂O₅   | 30 kg      | 60–80 kg    |
| K₂O    | 30 kg      | 80–120 kg   |
| S       | 25 kg      | 40 kg       |
| B       | 0.5 kg/ha  | 0.5 kg/ha   |

SPLIT APPLICATION OF NITROGEN:
- Basal: 25% N + Full P + Full K (at sowing)
- 30 DAS: 25% N (squaring stage)
- 60 DAS: 25% N (flowering)
- 90 DAS: 25% N (boll development)

FOLIAR SPRAY SCHEDULE:
- 2% DAP solution: At 60 and 80 DAS (boosts boll retention)
- 1% KCl + 0.5% KH₂PO₄: At boll development (quality improvement)
- 0.25% Boron: At flowering (prevents boll shedding)

FYM/Compost: 10–15 t/ha as basal (crucial for black cotton soil structure)

=======================================================================
SECTION 4: WATER MANAGEMENT
=======================================================================

CRITICAL IRRIGATION STAGES:
1. At sowing (if no rain)
2. Squaring (45 DAS)
3. Flowering (60–70 DAS) — most critical
4. Boll development (90–100 DAS)
5. Boll opening (120 DAS)

DRIP IRRIGATION (recommended for Gujarat):
- 60% water saving compared to flood irrigation
- Fertigation possible — improves fertilizer use efficiency
- Daily drip rate: 25–30 mm/day during flowering

SECTION 5: MAJOR PESTS OF COTTON
=======================================================================

5.1 BOLLWORMS — The Primary Pest Complex
Three species attack simultaneously:

A. AMERICAN BOLLWORM (Helicoverpa armigera):
IDENTIFICATION:
- Green/brown caterpillar with white stripes on body
- Half of larval body hangs outside the boll
- Attacks squares, flowers, and young bolls
- Can develop Bt resistance (very important for Vidarbha farmers)

MANAGEMENT:
- Pheromone traps: 5 traps/ha (Helilure lure, change every 21 days)
- Spinosad 45 SC: 125 ml/acre
- Chlorantraniliprole 18.5 SC: 150 ml/acre (most effective)
- Emamectin benzoate 5 SG: 80 g/acre
- NPV (Helicoverpa NPV): 500 LE/ha spray — biological, safe
- Do NOT spray during flowering (protect pollinators)

B. PINK BOLLWORM (Pectinophora gossypiella):
IDENTIFICATION:
- Pink caterpillar inside the boll (larvae enter boll, not seen outside)
- Two seeds joined by silk thread inside boll ("double seed")
- Boll has small circular entry hole

MANAGEMENT:
- Gossyplure pheromone traps: 5 traps/ha
- Profenofos 50 EC: 1 litre/acre spray at boll formation
- Bt spray (Bacillus thuringiensis var. kurstaki): 1 kg/acre
- Avoid late sowing (late crop more vulnerable)

C. SPOTTED BOLLWORM (Earias vittella):
- Attacks young shoots (shot-hole pattern), then bolls

5.2 SUCKING PESTS (Most important in early season)

WHITEFLY (Bemisia tabaci):
- Most serious pest in recent years — causes Cotton Leaf Curl Virus (CLCuV)
- YELLOWING, leaf curling, stunting — all caused by whitefly-transmitted virus
- ETL: 10 adults/leaf

MANAGEMENT:
- Yellow sticky traps: 10 traps/ha (early monitoring)
- Flonicamid 50 WG: 60 g/acre (most selective, safe for natural enemies)
- Spiromesifen 22.9 SC: 200 ml/acre
- Avoid Imidacloprid and Acephate (resistance developed)
- Silver reflective mulch: Repels whiteflies significantly

JASSIDS/LEAFHOPPERS (Amrasca biguttula):
- Yellow triangular wedge-shaped insects on leaf underside
- ETL: 2 per leaf
MANAGEMENT:
- Imidacloprid 17.8 SL: 150 ml/acre
- Acephate 75 SP: 400 g/acre

THRIPS (Thrips tabaci):
- Silver crinkling of leaves
MANAGEMENT:
- Spinosad 45 SC: 75 ml/acre

MEALYBUGS (Phenacoccus solenopsis):
- White waxy cottony mass on stems and leaves
- Spreads via ants — use ant barriers
MANAGEMENT:
- Profenofos 50 EC: 1 litre/acre
- Spray water jet to dislodge colonies
- Release Cryptolaemus montrouzieri (mealybug destroyer) — 10/plant

5.3 TOBACCO CATERPILLAR (Spodoptera litura)
- Cut leaves, skeletonize leaves in groups
- ETL: 1 egg mass/plant or 5% defoliation
MANAGEMENT:
- Chlorpyrifos 20 EC: 1.5 litre/acre
- NPV (SNPV): 500 LE/ha

SECTION 6: MAJOR DISEASES OF COTTON
=======================================================================

6.1 COTTON LEAF CURL VIRUS (CLCuV)
MOST SERIOUS DISEASE IN INDIA — No curative treatment.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Upward curling of leaves
- Thick dark green veins, twisted leaves
- Dark vein enations (swellings) on leaf underside
- Plant stunted; no boll formation

MANAGEMENT:
- Control whitefly vector (see above)
- Remove and destroy affected plants within 30 DAS
- Avoid CLCuV-susceptible varieties (HD 324 susceptible)
- Grow resistant varieties: HS 6, HD 432

6.2 BACTERIAL BLIGHT (Angular Leaf Spot) — Xanthomonas campestris
IDENTIFICATION:
- Angular water-soaked spots on leaves, turning brown
- Shot-hole appearance
- Black lesions on bolls (boll rot) in severe cases

MANAGEMENT:
- Seed treatment: Streptocycline 500 ppm solution
- Spray: Copper oxychloride 0.3% + Streptocycline 100 ppm
- Remove infected crop debris after harvest

6.3 FUSARIUM WILT (Fusarium oxysporum)
IDENTIFICATION:
- Sudden wilting of plant
- Brown discolouration of vascular tissue (cut stem — brown ring visible)
- More severe in light soils and repeated cotton cultivation

MANAGEMENT:
- Crop rotation (avoid cotton after cotton)
- Seed treatment with Trichoderma viride 4 g/kg seed
- Soil application: T. viride 5 kg/ha at sowing

6.4 ALTERNARIA LEAF BLIGHT
- Brown circular spots with yellow halo
- Worse after heavy rains
MANAGEMENT:
- Mancozeb 75 WP: 2 g/litre spray

=======================================================================
SECTION 7: INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT (IPM) CALENDAR
=======================================================================

PRE-SOWING:
- Deep summer ploughing (May–June) to destroy soil pests
- Apply FYM + Trichoderma

VEGETATIVE STAGE (0–45 DAS):
- Install pheromone traps
- Monitor for jassids, aphids
- No insecticide if natural enemies present

SQUARING STAGE (45–70 DAS):
- Scout for bollworm eggs
- Apply only at ETL

FLOWERING AND BOLL DEVELOPMENT (70–120 DAS):
- Monitor for pink bollworm
- Avoid spraying during peak flowering (9 AM – 12 PM)

BOLL OPENING (120+ DAS):
- Stop all pesticide applications
- Harvest only fully open bolls

=======================================================================
HARVEST AND POST-HARVEST:
=======================================================================
- Pick cotton early morning (dew removes natural colour)
- Separate grades: S-6, MCU-5, J-34 (different prices)
- Avoid storing wet cotton — aflatoxin contamination risk
- Minimum Support Price (MSP) 2024-25: Medium Staple ₹7,121/quintal,
  Long Staple ₹7,521/quintal
