ICAR COMPREHENSIVE AGROFORESTRY, BAMBOO & TIMBER SPECIES MANUAL
================================================================================
Grounded in ICAR-CAFRI (Central Agroforestry Research Institute, Jhansi) & NBM Norms.

SECTION 1: SCIENTIFIC AGROFORESTRY SYSTEMS & TIMBER SPECIES SELECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Agroforestry integrates woody perennials (trees/bamboo) with agricultural crops on the same land management unit, creating multi-tier canopy production, windbreaks that reduce crop desiccation by 25%, deep nutrient pumping from subsoil layers, carbon sequestration credits, and high capital returns upon timber maturity.

1. Poplar (Populus deltoides - The Gold Standard Agroforestry Tree for North India):
   - Suitable Regions: Punjab, Haryana, Western Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand Tarai, and North Bihar (above 28°N latitude with winter dormancy).
   - High-Yielding Clones: G-48, Udai, W-39, WSL-22, PL-5.
   - Planting Material: Entire Transplants (ETPs) - 1-year-old bare-root nursery saplings measuring 3.5 to 5.0 meters in height with healthy taproot.
   - Spacing Geometry:
     * Boundary Plantation: Single or double row along field bunds at 2.0 to 2.5 meters spacing.
     * Block Plantation: 5m x 4m (200 trees/acre) or 5m x 3m (266 trees/acre) or paired row 7m x 2m (allows tractor tillage for 5-6 years).
   - Rotation & Harvesting: Trees mature in 5 to 7 years, attaining 80 to 100 cm girth (diameter 25-32 cm). A single mature tree yields 350-500 kg commercial wood, fetching ₹3,500 to ₹5,500 per tree at Yamunanagar / Rampur plywood markets (netting ₹7 Lakh to ₹10 Lakh per acre).
   - Deciduous Leaf Advantage: Sheds all leaves completely during winter (November to February), allowing 100% solar penetration for high-yielding intercropped Rabi Wheat, Mustard, Potato, and Sugarcane!

2. Clonal Eucalyptus (Eucalyptus tereticornis - Plywood & Paper Pulp Clones):
   - High-Tech Clones: ITC Bhadrachalam Clones (Clone 3, 7, 10, 27, 413) propagated via rooted cuttings (free from seed genetic variation).
   - Growth & Rotation: Harvested in 4 to 5 years for paper pulp (short rotation, 3m x 1.5m spacing, 880 trees/acre) or 7 to 8 years for poles and plywood veneer logs (3m x 3m, 440 trees/acre).
   - Moisture Efficiency: Modern clones consume less water per gram of dry biomass produced than sugarcane or paddy! Plant along canal bunds or field boundaries as living windbreaks.

3. Melia dubia / Malabar Neem (Maha-Neem):
   - Adaptability: Thrives across Southern, Western, and Central India (Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, Gujarat, MP).
   - Characteristics: Extremely fast-growing soft-hardwood tree (reaches 35-40 feet height in 3 years); completely resistant to termites; high wood density (550 kg/m³) ideal for face veneer, pencil, and packing-case industries.
   - Spacing: 3m x 3m (block) or 4m x 4m (intercropped with pulses and turmeric).
   - Harvest: 5 to 6 years rotation producing 10-15 cubic feet wood per tree, trading at ₹400-₹500 per cubic foot.

4. Teak (Tectona grandis / Sagwan):
   - High-Value Long Rotation: Plant tissue-culture or root-shoot stump cuttings at 3m x 3m spacing. Requires well-drained alluvial deep soil.
   - Thinning & Harvest: Commercial intermediate thinning at 8th and 14th year (generates ₹3 Lakh/acre from poles); final timber felling at 20-25 years yields world-class A-grade structural timber valued at ₹2,500-₹4,000 per cubic foot.

SECTION 2: NATIONAL BAMBOO MISSION (NBM) & COMMERCIAL BAMBOO CULTIVATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bamboo is classified as a "grass" under the Indian Forest Act amendment, completely exempting bamboo grown on private agricultural farmland from forest felling permits and transit passes (Challan-free interstate transport).
- Commercial High-Biomass Species:
  * Bambusa balcooa (Bheema Bamboo - Heavy structural bamboo, thick wall, tissue-culture propagated).
  * Dendrocalamus strictus (Solid Male Bamboo - drought hardy, ideal for central India).
  * Bambusa tulda & Dendrocalamus asper (Giant Timber Bamboo for furniture, bamboo ply, and bio-coal pellets).
- Spacing & Clump Management:
  * High-Density Energy Plantation: 3m x 2m (660 plants/acre) for biomass power plants and ethanol production.
  * Timber / Furniture Poles: 4m x 4m (250 plants/acre) or 5m x 5m.
- Clump Maintenance:
  * Dig planting pits of 60 x 60 x 60 cm, fill with 15 kg FYM + 200g SSP + 20g Chlorpyrifos dust.
  * 1st Commercial Harvesting begins in 4th year; harvest 5-8 mature 3-year-old culms per clump annually, leaving younger shoots.
  * Perennial Yield: Continuous annual harvesting for 40 to 60 years without replanting! A 4-year-old plantation yields 20 to 30 tonnes green bamboo per acre annually, providing steady income of ₹80,000 to ₹1,20,000 per acre per year.
- National Bamboo Mission (NBM) Subsidies:
  * 50% government subsidy (up to ₹1,20,000 per hectare for general category, ₹1,50,000 for SC/ST and North-East states) for tissue-culture plantlets, drip irrigation installation, and first 3 years plantation maintenance. Apply on nbm.nic.in.

SECTION 3: INTERCROPPING COMPATIBILITY & CARBON CREDIT MONETIZATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Compatible Intercrops During Tree Immature Phase (Years 1 to 3):
   - Year 1 & 2: Summer Moong, Soybean, Groundnut, Cowpea, Tomato, Chilli, Wheat, Mustard, Garlic, Onion.
   - Year 3 & 4 (Shaded Understory Phase): Shade-tolerant commercial crops flourish under 40-60% canopy shade:
     * Turmeric (Prathibha, IISR Alleppey Supreme) — yields 8-10 tonnes/acre.
     * Ginger (IISR Varada, Nadia) — yields 6-8 tonnes/acre.
     * Colocasia (Arbi), Elephant Foot Yam (Suran - Gajendra variety), and Pineapple.
2. Agroforestry Carbon Credit Income:
   - Modern international carbon registries (Verra VCS, Gold Standard) issue Verified Carbon Units (VCUs) for agroforestry plantations sequestering atmospheric CO2.
   - An acre of Poplar or Melia dubia sequesters 10 to 18 tonnes CO2 equivalent per year. Farmers registered with FPO carbon aggregation programs receive annual carbon credit payouts of ₹2,500 to ₹5,000 per acre over and above their agricultural and timber revenues.
