ICAR TECHNICAL BULLETIN — Vegetable Cultivation Guide for Kerala Farmers
Source: Indian Council of Vegetable Research (ICAR-IIVR), Varanasi & Kerala Agriculture University
Document Reference: KAU/VEG/2024/Kerala-03

Kerala has excellent potential for vegetable cultivation due to its year-round warm climate,
but specific challenges like high rainfall, humidity, and pest pressure require adapted management.

=======================================================================
SECTION 1: SEASON-WISE VEGETABLE PLANTING CALENDAR FOR KERALA
=======================================================================

SUMMER SEASON (February–May):
Best time for cucurbits and gourds.

MONSOON SEASON (June–September):
Use raised beds. Leafy vegetables and beans work best.

POST-MONSOON (October–January):
Best quality tomatoes, brinjal, capsicum, chilly. Lowest pest pressure.

SECTION 2: KEY VEGETABLE VARIETIES FOR KERALA
=======================================================================

TOMATO:
- Sakthi: Suitable for Kerala climate, moderate disease resistance
- Mukthi: Heat tolerant, 60–65 days to harvest, 25–30 t/ha
- Arka Vikas: IIHR variety, indeterminate, 30–35 t/ha
- CO-3: Widely grown, suitable for monsoon (with support)
- Avoid: European varieties — not adapted to Kerala's high humidity

BITTERGOURD (Pavakka):
- Preethi: High yielding (18–20 t/ha), dark green fruit, 55 days
- Priya: Bitter type, preferred by consumers, 18 t/ha
- CO-1: Tamil Nadu variety, good for summer
- IIHR-K: Disease tolerant

SNAKEGOURD:
- CO-1, CO-2: 20–25 t/ha, suitable for trellising
- Thiruvananthapuram Local: Popular, long fruits (1–1.5 m)
- PLR-1: Palakkad local release, 30–35 t/ha

OKRA (Ladies Finger / Vendakka):
- Arka Anamika: High yielding, 15–18 t/ha, 50 days to first harvest
- Arka Abhay: Best for Kerala climate, YVMV resistant (important!)
- MDU-1: Good for high-density planting
- Avoid YVMV-susceptible varieties during whitefly season

COWPEA:
- Lola: Bushy type, 45 days to harvest, 15–18 t/ha green pods
- Bhaskar: Climbing type, 55 days, 20 t/ha
- Kanakamony: Local variety, high protein content

BRINJAL:
- Surya: Round purple, 25–30 t/ha, early bearing (60 days)
- Arka Shirish: Long purple, 35–40 t/ha, suitable for post-monsoon
- Muktakeshi: Local, preferred for culinary properties

CHILLY:
- K-1: High yielding, suitable for Kerala (hot and humid)
- Kanthari: Local bird's eye chilly, high market value
- CO-1: Compact, 45 t/ha fresh green chillies

SECTION 3: RAISED BED CULTIVATION (CRITICAL FOR KERALA MONSOON)
=======================================================================

WHY RAISED BEDS:
- Heavy rainfall in Kerala (2000–4000 mm) causes waterlogging
- Root diseases (Phytophthora, Pythium) are major causes of crop failure in flat beds
- Raised beds ensure proper drainage and aeration

HOW TO MAKE RAISED BEDS:
1. Plot size: 1 m wide × 6–8 m long (for home gardens: 1 m × 3 m)
2. Bed height: 30 cm above ground level during monsoon; 15 cm in dry season
3. Path width between beds: 50 cm (for movement and drainage)
4. Direction: Orient beds North–South for maximum sun exposure
5. Material: Use topsoil + 25% compost + 25% sand (for loose, well-drained mix)

SOIL MIXTURE FOR RAISED BEDS (per sq. metre):
- Red soil/loam: 50% (2 baskets)
- Well-decomposed compost/FYM: 25% (1 basket)
- Sand or coir pith: 25% (1 basket)
- Lime: 100 g (to adjust pH)
- Bone meal or rock phosphate: 100 g

MAINTENANCE:
- Drench beds with Copper oxychloride 0.25% before planting (soil sterilization)
- Trichoderma viride 50 g/sq.m mixed with compost
- Mulch with dry leaves or paddy straw (5 cm thick) to retain moisture and suppress weeds

SECTION 4: FERTILIZER RECOMMENDATIONS FOR VEGETABLES
=======================================================================

PER 10 SQUARE METRE RAISED BED:
- FYM or vermicompost: 25 kg (basal, mix into soil)
- Urea: 75 g (top dressing in 3 splits at 15, 30, 45 days)
- Superphosphate (SSP): 200 g (basal application)
- Muriate of Potash (MOP): 75 g (split: 50% basal, 50% at flowering)

PER HECTARE RECOMMENDATIONS:

| Vegetable   | N (kg) | P₂O₅ (kg) | K₂O (kg) | FYM (t) |
|------------|--------|-----------|---------|---------|
| Tomato     | 120    | 60        | 60      | 25      |
| Bittergourd| 75     | 50        | 75      | 20      |
| Brinjal    | 120    | 60        | 60      | 25      |
| Okra       | 90     | 45        | 45      | 15      |
| Cowpea     | 25*    | 60        | 30      | 10      |
| Chilly     | 100    | 60        | 60      | 25      |
| Ginger     | 75     | 50        | 75      | 30      |

* Cowpea fixes N — use low N fertilizer.

FOLIAR NUTRITION (spray to boost yield):
- Panchagavya 3% (weekly from 20 days onwards): Stimulates growth
- 19:19:19 NPK at 5 g/litre (weekly from flowering): Boosts fruit set
- Boron 0.1% (borax solution): At flower initiation — reduces fruit drop

SECTION 5: MAJOR VEGETABLE PESTS IN KERALA
=======================================================================

5.1 FRUIT BORER (Leucinodes orbonalis) — Brinjal/Tomato
Most damaging pest of brinjal in Kerala.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Pink larvae bore into fruits and shoots
- Entry hole with frass (excrement) visible on fruit surface
- Affected fruits are unmarketable
- Shoots wilt and dry up (shoot borer phase)

MANAGEMENT:
- ETL: 5% affected shoots or 2% bored fruits
- Spinosad 45 SC: 0.3 ml/litre spray
- Emamectin benzoate 5 SG: 0.4 g/litre
- Chlorantraniliprole 18.5 SC: 0.3 ml/litre (most effective)
- Pheromone traps: 10–12 traps/ha for monitoring
- Remove and destroy bored fruits regularly

5.2 WHITEFLY (Bemisia tabaci) — Vector of YVMV in Okra
Yellow Vein Mosaic Virus (YVMV) — the most devastating okra disease, spread by whitefly.

IDENTIFICATION OF YVMV:
- Yellow vein network pattern on leaves (distinct yellow veins, green between them)
- Leaves become yellow and severely stunted
- No cure — remove infected plants immediately

WHITEFLY MANAGEMENT:
- Use YVMV-resistant varieties (Arka Abhay, Arka Anamika)
- Yellow sticky traps: 25 traps/ha
- Imidacloprid 17.8 SL: 0.5 ml/litre (apply early when whiteflies first appear)
- Beauveria bassiana spray: 5 g/litre on leaf underside
- Reflective mulch (silver polyethylene): Disorients whiteflies

5.3 THRIPS (Thrips palmi, Scirtothrips dorsalis) — Cucurbits/Chilly
MANAGEMENT:
- Spinosad 45 SC: 0.3 ml/litre
- Fipronil 5 SC: 1 ml/litre
- Blue sticky traps: 25 traps/ha

5.4 APHIDS — Multiple crops
MANAGEMENT:
- Imidacloprid 17.8 SL: 0.5 ml/litre
- Neem oil 3%: 3 ml/litre + soap solution
- Lady bird beetles and parasitoids — conserve natural enemies

5.5 RED PUMPKIN BEETLE (Aulacophora foveicollis) — Cucurbits
IDENTIFICATION:
- Orange-red adult beetles on leaves and flowers
- Adults eat holes in leaves; larvae bore roots
- Most damaging in first 4 weeks after sowing

MANAGEMENT:
- Seed treatment: Carbendazim 1 g/kg seed
- Cover young seedlings with insect net (40 mesh) for 3–4 weeks
- Carbaryl 50 WP: 2 g/litre spray when beetles appear

SECTION 6: MAJOR VEGETABLE DISEASES IN KERALA
=======================================================================

6.1 LATE BLIGHT (Phytophthora infestans) — Tomato
Most serious disease of tomato in high-altitude Kerala (Wayanad, Idukki).

IDENTIFICATION:
- Water-soaked lesions on leaves, turning brown-black
- White sporulating growth on leaf underside in humid conditions
- Rapid collapse of entire plant
- Brown lesions on tomato fruits

MANAGEMENT:
- Metalaxyl + Mancozeb 72 WP: 3 g/litre spray
- Cymoxanil + Mancozeb 72 WP: 3 g/litre
- Copper oxychloride 50 WP: 3 g/litre (preventive)
- Do not irrigate by sprinkler — use drip
- Avoid planting in low-lying, poorly-drained areas

6.2 DOWNY MILDEW — Cucurbits
IDENTIFICATION:
- Yellow angular patches on upper leaf surface
- Grayish-purple sporulation on underside

MANAGEMENT:
- Fosetyl-Al 80 WP: 2.5 g/litre
- Dimethomorph + Mancozeb: 3 g/litre
- Space plants widely for air circulation

6.3 POWDERY MILDEW — Cucurbits/Okra
IDENTIFICATION:
- White powdery coating on leaves
- Worse in dry conditions with high humidity nights

MANAGEMENT:
- Wettable sulphur 80 WP: 3 g/litre spray
- Hexaconazole 5 EC: 1 ml/litre
- Avoid overhead irrigation in evening

6.4 DAMPING-OFF — All seedlings
The number one cause of seedling mortality in Kerala nurseries.

CAUSE:
- Pythium, Phytophthora, Fusarium fungi
- Triggered by overwatering, poor drainage, heavy rain

PREVENTION (most important):
- Raised beds (mandatory)
- Drenching with Copper oxychloride 0.25% before sowing
- Mix Trichoderma viride 50 g/sq.m with growing medium
- Avoid overwatering seedlings

TREATMENT:
- Drench with Metalaxyl + Mancozeb 72 WP 3 g/litre
- Reduce watering frequency

SECTION 7: PROTECTED CULTIVATION (SHADENET / POLYHOUSE)
=======================================================================

BENEFITS:
- Year-round production — not limited by monsoon
- 30–40% higher yields
- Reduced pesticide use (insect exclusion)
- Premium price for off-season produce

TYPES SUITABLE FOR KERALA:
1. Shadenet House (50% shade): Best for leafy vegetables, nurseries, orchids
2. Low-cost polyhouse (UV-film): For tomato, capsicum, cucumber
3. Net House (40-mesh insect net): For organic, pesticide-free vegetables

GOVERNMENT SUBSIDY:
- MIDH (Mission for Integrated Development of Horticulture): 50% subsidy for polyhouse
- Maximum area: 4000 sq.m for small farmers
- Apply through District Horticulture Office

=======================================================================
PEST MANAGEMENT CALENDAR FOR VEGETABLES (Kerala-Specific)
=======================================================================

PRE-SOWING:
- Soil solarization with clear polythene (1 month in March–April)
- Soil drench with Trichoderma

SEEDLING STAGE:
- Damping-off prevention: Drenching
- Aphid/whitefly monitoring: Yellow sticky traps

VEGETATIVE STAGE:
- Scout weekly for thrips, aphids, leaf miners
- Spray only when ETL is crossed

FLOWERING AND FRUITING:
- Fruit borer monitoring: Pheromone traps
- Spray during early morning or evening (avoid bee-active hours)
- Observe Pre-Harvest Intervals strictly

POST-HARVEST:
- Remove and compost crop debris
- Deep plough to expose soil pests
