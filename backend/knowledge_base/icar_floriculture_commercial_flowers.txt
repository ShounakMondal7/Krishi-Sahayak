SECTION 1: MARIGOLD (GENDA) COMMERCIAL PRODUCTION
=======================================================================
1. Major Commercial Varieties:
   - African Marigold (Tagetes erecta - Bold Flowers): Pusa Narangi Gainda (orange, high lutein content), Pusa Basanti Gainda (sulfur yellow), Bidhan Marigold-1, Arka Bangara, Maxima Yellow.
   - French Marigold (Tagetes patula - Small bedding/loose flowers): Pusa Arpita, Red Brocade, Rusty Red.

2. Nursery Raising & Seasonal Calendar:
   - Seed Rate: 1.0 to 1.5 kg per hectare for open-pollinated varieties; 200 to 250 g/ha for F1 hybrids.
   - Sowing Windows:
     * Rainy Season Crop: Sow May–June, transplant June–July, harvest August–October (Puja & Dussehra festival demand).
     * Winter Season Crop: Sow August–September, transplant September–October, harvest November–February (Diwali & Wedding peak).
     * Summer Season Crop: Sow January–February, transplant February–March, harvest April–June.

3. The Mandatory "Pinching" (Apical Decapitation) Technique:
   - Critical Timing: Exactly 30 to 35 days after transplanting when plants are 15 to 20 cm tall and have developed 6 to 8 pairs of leaves.
   - Method: Pinch off 2 to 3 cm of the terminal vegetative apical shoot with clean fingernails or shears.
   - Physiological Benefit: Breaks apical auxin dominance, triggering the emergence of 25 to 35 vigorous side branches (laterals), increasing total flower yield per plant by over 80%.

4. Fertilizer & 15-Liter Knapsack Foliar Nutrition:
   - Basal Dressing: 20 tonnes FYM + 60 kg N, 80 kg P2O5, 80 kg K2O per hectare.
   - Top Dressing: 60 kg N applied in two equal splits at 30 and 45 days after transplanting.
   - Foliar Booster Spray (Pre-Flowering Stage): Spray water-soluble NPK 19:19:19 at 75 grams per 15-Liter pump (5g/L) combined with Micronutrient mixture (Zinc + Boron) at 30 ml per 15L pump to enhance flower diameter, petals thickness, and vibrant orange pigment.

SECTION 2: ROSE (GULAB) & GLADIOLUS CULTIVATION
=======================================================================
1. Commercial Cut & Loose Rose Management:
   - Varieties: Gladiator, Grand Gala, First Red, Passion, Taj Mahal, Dutch Red (Greenhouse cut-flowers); Gruss an Teplitz, Desi Gulab, Edward Rose (Loose flower distillation & garlands).
   - North India Pruning Schedule: Exactly during the second week of October (October 10–20).
     * Cut back mature canes to 45–60 cm above ground level, retaining 4 to 5 strong outward-facing buds.
     * Cut at an angle of 45° about 6 mm above an outward bud to prevent water pooling on cuts.
   - Die-Back Disease Prevention: Immediately swab every cut stem surface with Copper Oxychloride paste (COC 50% WP mixed with linseed oil) to prevent fungal invasion (Diplodia rosarum).

2. Gladiolus (Sword Lily) Spike Production:
   - Commercial Varieties: White Prosperity, Priscilla, Nova Lux, American Beauty, Arka Amar, Punjab Glance.
   - Corm Specifications: Medium to large corms (4.5 to 5.5 cm diameter) free from scab or neck rot.
   - Planting & Spacing: Furrow planting at 30 cm × 20 cm spacing, 7 to 8 cm deep.
   - Spike Harvesting: For distant markets, harvest flower spikes in early morning when the lowest 1 or 2 florets show color but have not opened. Place immediately in clean buckets with water.

SECTION 3: TUBEROSE (RAJNIGANDHA) & CHRYSANTHEMUM (GULDAUDI)
=======================================================================
1. Tuberose (Polianthes tuberosa):
   - Varieties:
     * Single Flowered (Highly fragrant, for essential oil concrete & loose flowers): Prajwal, Shringar, Phule Rajani.
     * Double Flowered (Multilayer petals, for cut-flower vases & bouquets): Vaibhav, Suvasini, Pearl Double.
   - Bulb Planting: Spindle-shaped bulbs of 2.5 to 3.0 cm diameter planted in February–March or June–July at 30 cm × 20 cm spacing.
   - Flower Yield: 8 to 10 tonnes of loose flowers or 1.5 to 2.0 lakh cut spikes per hectare annually.

2. Chrysanthemum (Guldaudi):
   - Varieties: Pusa Shanti, Pusa Anmol, Baggi, Ratlam Selection, Indira, Mother Teresa.
   - Propagation: Terminal stem cuttings (5–7 cm long) taken in June–July, treated with IBA (1000 ppm) root hormone powder, and rooted in coarse sand nursery beds.
   - Disbudding for Standard Exhibition Blooms: Remove all side flower buds, retaining only the central terminal bud to produce giant, show-quality flowers.

SECTION 4: PEST & DISEASE MANAGEMENT WITH 15L SPRAY DOSAGES
=======================================================================
1. Flower Thrips & Mites (Causes Petal Browning & Curling):
   - Symptoms: Microscopic thrips rasp petal margins, causing dark scorched edges and flower distortion.
   - Chemical Control: Spray Fipronil 5% SC at 30 ml per 15-Liter pump (2 ml/L) OR Spinosad 45% SC at 7.5 ml per 15-Liter pump (0.5 ml/L). Alternate with Neem Oil 1500 ppm at 45 ml per 15L pump.

2. Alternaria Leaf & Flower Blight (Alternaria tagetica / dianthi):
   - Symptoms: Brown necrotic circular spots on leaves spreading to flower sepals and petals during humid weather.
   - Chemical Control: Spray Mancozeb 75% WP at 35 grams per 15-Liter pump (2.5 g/L) OR Azoxystrobin 18.2% + Difenoconazole 11.4% SC at 15 ml per 15-Liter pump (1 ml/L).

SECTION 5: POST-HARVEST HANDLING & VASE-LIFE PRESERVATION
=======================================================================
1. Floral Pulsing Solution (Doubles Cut-Flower Vase Life):
   - Prepare clean holding solution: Distilled or clean soft water containing 2% Sucrose (table sugar: 20 g/liter) + 200 ppm 8-Hydroxyquinoline Citrate (8-HQC: 200 mg/L) or Citric acid (150 mg/L) to lower water pH to 3.5–4.0.
   - Stand cut gladiolus, rose, or tuberose stems in pulsing solution for 12 hours at cool temperature (15–20°C) before packing. Extends indoor vase display life from 5 days up to 12–14 days.
