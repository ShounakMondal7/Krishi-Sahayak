SECTION 1: BROWN PLANTHOPPER (BPH) — Nilaparvata lugens
=======================================================================

Brown Planthopper (BPH) is the most destructive insect pest of rice in Kerala, causing "hopperburn" — complete death of plants in circular patches.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Small (3–4 mm), brown insects at the base of rice plants
- Both macropterous (long-winged) and brachypterous (short-winged) forms
- Nymphs are pale yellow-brown
- "Hopperburn": circular patches of dead, dried plants resembling fire damage
- Heavy honeydew secretion leads to sooty mould growth

MONITORING:
- Use yellow sticky traps (1 per 10 ha)
- Sample base of 5 random hills per plot
- Economic Threshold Level (ETL): 10 BPH/hill during vegetative stage; 20 BPH/hill during reproductive stage

MANAGEMENT:
1. Chemical Control (spray at base of plants):
   - Buprofezin 25 SC: 1 ml/litre (IGR — insect growth regulator, safest)
   - Thiamethoxam 25 WG: 0.5 g/litre (rotate to prevent resistance)
   - Pymetrozine 50 WG: 0.3 g/litre
   - Imidacloprid 17.8 SL: 0.5 ml/litre (use only 1–2 times per season)
   - Chlorantraniliprole 18.5 SC: 0.4 ml/litre
   - NEVER use organophosphates (Quinalphos, Profenophos) — resurgence risk

2. Resistance Management:
   - Rotate insecticides with different modes of action each application
   - NEVER apply the same class of insecticide twice consecutively
   - Avoid blanket spraying — spray only affected areas

3. Cultural Control:
   - Plant BPH-resistant varieties: Ptb-33, IR-64, Swarna, Jyothi
   - Avoid excess nitrogen (promotes BPH population buildup)
   - Use alternate wetting and drying (AWD) irrigation
   - Maintain proper spacing to reduce humidity

4. Biological Control:
   - Preserve natural enemies: spiders, water bugs, Cyrtorhinus lividipennis
   - Apply Beauveria bassiana 1.15 WP @ 2.5 kg/ha
   - Avoid broad-spectrum insecticides that kill natural enemies

IMPORTANT: Hopperburn is irreversible once it sets in. Prevention and early action are critical.

SECTION 2: LEAF FOLDER (Cnaphalocrocis medinalis)
=======================================================================

Leaf folder causes significant yield reduction by destroying leaf area needed for photosynthesis.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Larvae fold single rice leaf lengthwise and feed from inside
- White streaks (scraped leaf surface) running lengthwise on leaves
- Multiple folded leaves visible across field
- Adult moth: light brown with three black-bordered yellow stripes

ETL: 1–2 rolled leaves/hill (vegetative) or 2–5% tillers with rolled leaves

MANAGEMENT:
1. Chemical Control:
   - Chlorpyrifos 20 EC: 2.5 ml/litre
   - Monocrotophos 36 WSC: 1.6 ml/litre (restricted use)
   - Lambda-cyhalothrin 5 EC: 0.5 ml/litre
   - Cartap hydrochloride 50 SP: 1 g/litre

2. Cultural Control:
   - Clip and destroy leaf tips of seedlings before transplanting
   - Avoid excessive nitrogen
   - Light traps attract adults (1 trap/2 ha)

SECTION 3: STEM BORER (Scirpophaga incertulas — Yellow Stem Borer)
=======================================================================

Yellow Stem Borer causes "dead heart" in vegetative stage and "white ear" in reproductive stage.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Dead Heart: Central shoot dries and turns pale yellow; can be pulled out easily
- White Ear: Panicle fails to fill; grains become white/chalky
- Entry holes visible on stems; frass (excrement) inside stem

ETL: 5% dead hearts (vegetative); 1% white ears (reproductive)

MANAGEMENT:
1. Chemical Control:
   - Fipronil 0.3 GR: Apply @ 33 kg/ha to standing water
   - Cartap hydrochloride 4 G: 18.75 kg/ha granule application
   - Chlorantraniliprole 0.4 GR: 10 kg/ha

2. Pheromone Traps:
   - Set up 5 pheromone traps per hectare for monitoring
   - Replace lures every 3–4 weeks

3. Cultural Control:
   - Timely transplanting (avoid peak moth periods)
   - Clip seedling tips to remove egg masses before transplanting
   - Maintain continuous standing water (reduces pupation)

SECTION 4: GALL MIDGE (Orseolia oryzae)
=======================================================================

Gall midge is a sporadic but devastating pest in Kerala's Kuttanad and Palakkad regions.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Infested tillers form hollow, silver-coloured tubes ("silver shoots")
- Silver shoots are tubular, silvery, and 20–50 cm long
- The central shoot of the tiller is destroyed
- Rice plants tiller excessively to compensate

ETL: 5% silver shoots

MANAGEMENT:
1. Chemical Control:
   - Carbofuran 3 G: Apply 33 kg/ha in standing water (2–3 cm) at active infestation
   - Fipronil 5 SC: 1.5 ml/litre foliar spray
   - Chlorpyrifos 20 EC: 2.5 ml/litre

2. Cultural Control:
   - Early transplanting (mid-June for Virippu)
   - Use tolerant varieties: Vikas, Seeraga Samba, Ptb-33
   - Drain fields for 3–4 days to reduce larval survival

=======================================================================
SECTION 5: WHORL MAGGOT (Hydrellia philippina)
=======================================================================

Affects young seedlings and early transplanted rice.

IDENTIFICATION:
- Yellowing of young leaves (central leaf first)
- Narrow, elongated feeding marks on leaves
- Maggots (white, legless, 3 mm) visible inside leaf whorl

MANAGEMENT:
- Cultural: Raise nursery on slightly elevated seedbeds
- Chemical: Malathion 50 EC 0.05% spray on nursery

=======================================================================
SECTION 6: RICE HISPA (Dicladispa armigera)
=======================================================================

IDENTIFICATION:
- Black, spiny adult beetles scrape upper surface of leaves
- White linear marks on leaves parallel to midrib
- Larvae mine inside leaves creating tubular mines

MANAGEMENT:
- Clip and destroy infested leaves
- Quinalphos 25 EC: 2 ml/litre (when infestation is severe)
- Neem oil 3%: 3 ml/litre

=======================================================================
SECTION 7: INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT (IPM) CALENDAR
=======================================================================

PRE-PLANTING:
- Dry cultivation to expose soil pests to sun
- Incorporate previous crop stubble deeply

NURSERY STAGE:
- Seed treatment: Chlorpyrifos 20 EC (2.5 ml/litre) for 10 minutes, then dry
- Remove egg masses from seedling leaves

TRANSPLANTING (0–15 DAT):
- Apply Carbofuran 3G for soil pests and gall midge prevention
- Set up pheromone traps for stem borer monitoring

TILLERING (15–45 DAT):
- Scout for BPH weekly (check plant base)
- Monitor for leaf folder symptoms
- Apply insecticide only when ETL is reached

PANICLE INITIATION (45–60 DAT):
- Scout for white ears (stem borer)
- Check for BPH resurgence

GRAIN FILLING (60–90 DAT):
- Minimize pesticide use (consumer safety)
- Biological control preferred
- Watch for false smut and store grain mites

=======================================================================
PESTICIDE SAFETY GUIDELINES
=======================================================================

- Always wear protective equipment (gloves, mask, goggles) during spraying
- Do not spray during windy conditions or when bees are active
- Observe Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): stop all spraying 2 weeks before harvest
- Store pesticides in original containers away from food, water, and children
- Dispose of empty containers safely — do not burn or bury near water sources
- For poisoning emergencies: call 1800-180-1551 (Pesticide Helpline)
